02 — Section rhythm

Whitespace as hierarchy #rhythm

Spacing between sections is the loudest signal on a page. Three rhythms: tight, comfortable, airy. Pick one per surface and hold it.

tight --space-8 · 32
comfortable --space-12 · 48
airy --space-20 · 80
hero-airy --space-24 · 96
tight = dense admin · comfortable = default product · airy = marketing · hero-airy = launch heroes
Inside a card / form--space-3 to --space-6
Use for label → input → helper stacks and card body padding.
Don't mix the inside-rhythm with the between-section rhythm; cards collapse visually.
Between sections--space-12 to --space-24
Use between distinct content blocks: hero → features → social proof.
Don't drop below 32 between sections — readers stop reading them as sections.
Hierarchy by spacingwhitespace > size
Use a bigger gap above a heading than below — group heading + body by proximity.
Don't rely on font size alone for hierarchy. Spacing does more work.

05 — Column-split rules

1 vs 2 vs 3 #rules

When to split a page, when not to, and which side gets which job.

1 columnsingle track
Use for prose, mobile, modal bodies, anything that reads top-to-bottom without comparison.
Cap line length at 70–90 characters (~720px max-width). Past that, the eye loses the next line.
2 columnsmain + rail OR equal
Use when one piece supports the other (rail) OR when two items deserve equal weight (split). Most product pages.
Don't split body copy into 2 columns. That's print, not screen — readers can't tell which column continues where.
3 columnsnav · main · rail
Use for app shells where all three are persistent surfaces (IDE, design tool, email client).
Avoid for public pages — three persistent columns leave no breathing room on a laptop.
Left vs right railscan order matters
Left rail for navigation. Right rail for supporting content (summary, related, TOC).
Never put navigation on the right or summaries on the left — readers learn left = where I am, right = what's nearby.
Reading width~65ch
Body copy at 60–75 characters per line. Long-form caps at max-width: 65ch.
Don't stretch text the full width of a wide container. Cap it; let the container be wider than the text.

Mobile-first@media (min-width: ...)
Default styles are mobile. Add layers as the viewport grows — never the other way around.
Avoid max-width media queries except for true overrides (e.g. disabling sticky below md).
Tables on mobilescroll or stack
Use horizontal scroll for > 4-column tables. Use card-per-row for ≤ 4 columns of related fields.
Don't hide columns silently. If a column matters, surface it; if it doesn't, drop it everywhere.
Sticky on mobileusually drop
Drop most sticky behavior on phones — sticky elements eat thumb-zone real estate.
Keep only the primary action footer (checkout, form save) sticky on mobile.

Control density--size-* heights
Comfortable uses --size-md (36) controls. Compact uses --size-sm (28) for tables, lists, dense sidebars.
Never compact on touch — md auto-promotes to 44px on coarse pointers; preserve that.
Vertical rhythmone rhythm per surface
Pick the rhythm for the surface and hold it. Mixing comfortable cards with compact tables on the same page looks accidental.
Avoid letting individual components decide their own density. The surface decides.
Density by audiencemap to context
Marketing & consumer → airy. Product UI → comfortable. Power tools → compact with a "comfortable mode" toggle.
Don't ship compact as the default — make the user opt in.

How to generate three directions

Keep the components identical. Keep the tokens identical. Vary three things only: the primary/secondary emphasis (which content leads), the region split (hero + tabs vs. main + rail vs. single column), and the section rhythm (comfortable vs. airy vs. tight). Never vary the design language. This is the sanctioned way to make pages feel distinct without breaking consistency.

vary · emphasis vary · region split vary · section rhythm keep · components keep · tokens keep · type scale
