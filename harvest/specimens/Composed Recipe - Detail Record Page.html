Composed Recipe · Detail / record page · one skin, five compositions

Same skin. Five ways to read a record.

One record — a customer account with its orders, properties, and history — laid out five ways. Every frame wears the same neutral skin; what changes is purely composition and interaction: where the metadata sits, how you navigate sections, how the history reads. The skin is the paint, the composition is the skeleton.

Composition 1
Hero + tabs

PULLS — full-width record hero (icon + title + meta chips + status badge + primary/secondary actions + kebab) · horizontal tab nav (Overview / Activity / Files / Notes) · Overview body with info cards + a related-orders list.  WHY — the most legible default: the hero answers “what am I looking at” at a glance, and tabs keep a deep record from overwhelming the first view.

Composition 2
Two-column + side rail

PULLS — main content column (field sections, line-items table, totals) + sticky right side rail (status, owner, metadata as a definition list, quick actions, related links).  WHY — the classic record layout: the body holds the substance while the rail keeps status and the most-referenced properties pinned in view as you scroll.

Composition 3
Master-detail inline

PULLS — a narrow record list on the left (siblings) + the selected record's full detail on the right, with an inline-editable field and a kebab action menu.  WHY — for triage across many similar records (tickets, contacts, orders): you keep context of the set on the left and read or edit one on the right without a full page change.

Composition 4
Document / anchored

PULLS — a single centered column, long-form, with a sticky left anchored sub-nav that scrolls to sections · reads like a structured profile/document.  WHY — when a record is meant to be read end-to-end (a profile, a case file), document flow with a persistent outline beats fragmented tabs for comprehension.

Composition 5
Summary + activity timeline

PULLS — a compact summary header, then a prominent vertical activity / audit timeline as the main content (actor + time + change), plus a related-items list panel.  WHY — for records where the history is the point (audit logs, cases, deals): the timeline leads, the summary frames it, and the related panel offers the next hop.

Skins & how to apply them

A skin is nothing more than a small set of color + font tokens. Pick ONE skin per product and apply it across whichever compositions you choose — the composition is the skeleton, the skin is the paint. Every composition above is drawn in the Neutral skin; swap the token row and the same skeletons render coherently in any of the others.
