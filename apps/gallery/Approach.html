Lookbook · Chapter 00

Approach.

The reason most sites look the same isn't the components — it's that the project started without a brief. This chapter is the layer above the system: discovery, audit, and how to translate what you learn into which dials to turn before you open the editor.

01 — Discovery

The brief, in 12 questions #brief

One call. Ninety minutes. These twelve questions in this order. Each one closes off a part of the design space — by the end you know which Personality preset to load, which type pair to start from, and which page archetypes to plan.

01
Who is the visitor and what are they hoping to do in the first 10 seconds?
scopeIf you can't answer this, every page-rank decision later will be a coin flip.
02
What's the single primary action the site has to drive — and how do you measure success in 90 days?
goalSign-up, demo request, deposit, app-store install. One. Pricing CTA next to a careers link is a failure mode.
03
What's the existing brand maturity? Wordmark + tokens + voice doc, just a logo, or starting from scratch?
identityDetermines whether Chapter 07 (Identity) is a half-day or two weeks. Tell the client up front.
04
Three sites the client admires (any industry) — and one sentence each on why.
tasteThe "why" matters more than the "what." Three sites that all share "feels confident" point at a personality dial; three sites that all share "uses a lot of orange" point at a token.
05
One site they explicitly don't want to look like — and why.
anti-tasteThe exclusions tighten the brief faster than the inclusions. Worth two affirmative references.
06
Who's the audience tone-wise? Founders, ops managers, designers, consumers, regulated industries?
voiceRoutes you to a column on the brand-voice axis. Don't write copy in the wrong voice and hope the client will tell you.
07
What real photography or imagery do they have today — and can you see it before the kickoff ends?
imageryIf the answer is "we'll get stock later," budget a content strategy line item. Stock without direction sinks more sites than bad type.
08
What pages and flows are in scope, and which are out — explicitly?
scopeWrite the out-of-scope list down. "Just the homepage" never means just the homepage.
09
Who's the decider, who's the gatekeeper, and who has veto?
politicsIf the founder is the decider and Marketing is the gatekeeper, send drafts to Marketing and finals to the founder.
10
What's the launch date and what's hard about it (event, fundraise, end of quarter)?
scheduleA launch tied to a fundraise is a different project than a launch tied to a Friday.
11
Who builds it after I hand off — a dev team, a no-code tool, the client themselves?
handoffIf the builder is Webflow, design for it. If it's a Next.js team, ship tokens. If it's the client, build them an editable kit.
12
What would make you feel embarrassed if this site shipped that way?
guardrailsThe most useful answer you'll get all call. Write it on the kickoff one-pager and keep it visible the whole project.
Skip a question and you'll guess at the answer later — usually wrong, usually the night before launch.
02 — Existing-site audit

What to look at on an inherited site #audit

If they already have a site, audit it before you redesign. Four lenses: information architecture, content, brand, performance. One page per lens; tick the items as you go.

A — Information architecture

Does the structure match the goal?

  • Sitemap fits on one page — count the pages
  • Primary nav has ≤ 7 items
  • Footer doesn't duplicate the primary nav
  • Every page has one clear job (state it in 5 words)
  • No orphan pages from old campaigns
  • Search exists if the catalog is > 50 items
  • Breadcrumbs make sense at depth ≥ 3
B — Content

Does what's written earn its slot?

  • Homepage hero says what the product does in 8 words
  • Feature copy describes a benefit, not a feature
  • No "lorem" — and no placeholder team photos
  • Copy uses sentence case, not Title Case Everywhere
  • CTAs are verbs the user would actually say
  • Testimonials name a person + a role
  • Pricing page answers "what do I lose by not upgrading?"
C — Brand

Is there a system, or just a logo?

  • Single accent color used consistently for the primary action
  • Type pair holds across pages (display + body don't drift)
  • Imagery feels like it's from the same shoot or library
  • Icons share a stroke weight and family
  • Spacing rhythm reads as one rhythm (it doesn't on most sites)
  • 404 and empty states feel made, not generic
  • Mobile feels designed, not squeezed
D — Performance & basics

Will the design actually load?

  • LCP under 2.5s on a throttled 4G run
  • Hero image is < 200kb, served as AVIF/WebP
  • Font payload is < 100kb total (subset + woff2)
  • No layout shift on hero load
  • Keyboard nav works through primary tasks
  • Color contrast passes AA for body text
  • Reduced-motion doesn't break any animation

How to use this table

Don't pick a single row and stop. Pick a row as your starting point, then check whether one dial from another row earns the swap. A Technical product with a Playful motion vocabulary is interesting; a Technical product with all six Playful dials is a costume.

The rule

Five of six dials live in the same column. The sixth is the wildcard — the moment that makes this client's site different from the next Technical product or the next Editorial publication.

05 — Engagement size

Three scope tiers #tiers

Three honest envelopes for client work. Pick one before you write the proposal — if the client wants the middle list at the bottom-tier price, that's the conversation.

Refresh

1–2 weeks

Existing brand, existing structure. New tokens, new type, new homepage + one supporting page. No re-IA.

  • Token swap (color · type · radius)
  • Homepage redesign
  • One supporting page (about or pricing)
  • Mobile pass for both
  • New IA · sitemap rewrite
  • Photography direction
  • Marketing pages × N
  • Component library handoff

Rebuild

4–8 weeks

The default tier. New design system on existing brand. Full marketing site, no app chrome. The shape most clients actually need.

  • Full token kit · light + dark
  • 5–8 marketing pages
  • Blog index + article template
  • Mobile across all surfaces
  • Photography direction + 1 shoot brief
  • Handoff (tokens + Figma or code)
  • Brand identity from scratch
  • In-product UI

Reposition

3–6 months

Brand + system + site + app chrome. The only tier where "everything" is a fair word. Worth it if the company is at an inflection point.

  • Brand strategy + identity
  • Wordmark + lockups + app icon
  • Full design system + tokens
  • Marketing site (10+ pages)
  • App shell + key product surfaces
  • Photography shoot + asset library
  • Voice doc + microcopy lib
  • Engineering handoff package
06 — Kickoff doc

The one-pager you write after the brief #kickoff

Pin this on the project Notion. Refer back to it weekly. If a decision contradicts something on this page, that's the moment to call it out — not in the review.

The visitor

One sentence. Who shows up and what are they trying to do?

Solo developer evaluating analytics tools, comparing pricing pages on a Wednesday afternoon.

The primary action

One thing the site has to drive. One metric.

Start free trial → reach 200 signups/month within 90 days.

The shape

Which of the five sitemap patterns is this site?

Product marketing + Docs (hybrid). Marketing site at root; docs at /docs.

The archetype

Which row of the brand → dials table?

Technical with one Playful dial (the motion vocabulary).

The wildcard

The one dial that makes this site not look like the row above.

Bouncy stagger on the feature grid — the only spring curve in the whole site.

The references

Three liked, one disliked. One sentence each on why.

Like: Linear (calm density), Stripe (type rhythm), Plain (color restraint). Dislike: Generic dashboard SaaS (over-decorated empty states).

The embarrassment guardrail

What would make us regret shipping?

Looking like a Tailwind template. Default gradient buttons. Stock photos of people pointing at laptops.

Out of scope

What we are not building, written down.

In-product UI, login flow, customer dashboard, mobile app store assets, email templates.
Why this doc exists

Halfway through every project, someone forgets what was decided in the brief. The kickoff one-pager is the receipt. When a reviewer says "can we make it more colorful?" you point at the wildcard line and ask which other dial they want to trade.
