Lookbook · Chapter 09

Imagery.

The chapter that decides whether a site feels alive or templated. Components are silent here — what matters is what's in the picture, how it's treated, how it's cropped, and what fills the space while it loads. Visuals in this chapter are CSS-rendered placeholders; in production each becomes a real photograph or illustration.

01 — Subject matter

What's in the picture #subject

Five subject categories. Pick a primary and a secondary; the rest stay off the site. Most projects fail by trying all five at once — the site reads as a stock library instead of a brand.

Producttight
Peoplecandid
Placeenvironment
Abstracttexture
DocumentUI · screenshot
Productobjects · packaging · UI screenshots
Use when the product is the story. Tight crops, controlled background, consistent lighting.
Avoid the hero-on-blank-white shot — it reads as e-commerce, not brand.
Peoplecandid · environmental portrait
Use when the brand is people-facing or wants warmth. Candids over posed. Real users over models.
Avoid stock people pointing at laptops, generic diversity sampling, white-toothed handshakes.
Placeenvironment · architecture · landscape
Use for travel, real-estate, hospitality, fintech ("trust"), or when the product is invisible.
Avoid drone-shot mountain ranges as a metaphor for software. Be literal.
Abstracttexture · 3D · generative
Use for technical, infrastructure, or pre-product brands. Renders earn their place when the product isn't shippable yet.
Avoid the default "swirling 3D blob" — every dev tool launched in 2023 used it. The genericness is the problem.
DocumentUI screenshot · code · diagram
Use for tools where seeing the interface is the pitch. Show the product, not a logo on a marketing card.
Avoid blurry "actual product UI" thumbnails — they undersell. Re-shoot at 2x or design a clean specimen frame.
02 — Treatment

Six treatment recipes #treatment

One subject, six post-processing recipes. Each gives the same source image a different brand register. Pick one per project and apply it across every photo so the site reads as one shoot, even if it's six.

Naturalno filter
Warm gradeamber lift
Cool gradeblue lift
Duotoneaccent ↔ secondary
Desat + accent popspot color
Masked vignettesubject only
Naturalno post-treatment
Default when the photography is genuinely high-quality and on-brand. Trust the source.
Don't reach for natural just because you don't want to commit to a grade. Pick one.
Color gradewarm · cool · neutral
Use a consistent grade across every photo. Warm reads as human, premium, considered. Cool reads as technical, calm, scientific.
Don't mix grades. One site, one grade — even if the photos came from different sources.
Duotoneaccent ↔ secondary
Use when source quality varies wildly and you need a great unifier. Or as a deliberate strong brand register.
Don't duotone product UI screenshots — the colors stop carrying information. Duotone is for photos only.
Desat + accent popspot color on subject
Use when the brand color is the story. Photo recedes; accent leads.
Don't use on faces — desaturated skin tones look corpse-like. Subjects only.
Masked vignetteisolate the subject
Use for portraits or single-object shots where the background distracts. Fade to the surface color, not to black.
Don't mask architecture or landscape — the negative space is what makes the photo work.
03 — Aspect ratios

How the photo is cropped #ratios

Five aspect ratios cover almost every use. Standardize them in the system so the photographer (or you) shoots with the crops in mind, not against them.

21:9cinema · hero band
16:9video · hero · feature
4:3card · grid · index
1:1avatar · social · grid
3:4portrait · editorial
21:9 cinematichero bands · scroll-driven panoramas
Use sparingly — once per page max. A panoramic hero is a powerful first impression; two is wasted scroll.
Don't crop a portrait subject to 21:9 — you'll cut the head off. Reserve for environment / abstract.
16:9default media ratio
Default for hero, feature blocks, embedded video, opengraph. If in doubt, this.
Don't use for index cards — too wide, leaves dead space below the image.
4:3classic grid
Default for card grids, blog index, gallery. Reads as photography, not video.
Don't mix 4:3 and 16:9 in the same grid — the rows misalign.
1:1 squareavatars · OG · masonry
Use for avatars, social posts, masonry blocks that need a strong rhythmic beat.
Don't hero in 1:1 — squares don't lead the eye anywhere.
3:4 portraiteditorial · team pages
Use for portraits, team pages, editorial layouts that want a magazine register.
Don't use portrait crops in a tight nav or card grid — they punch holes vertically.
04 — Composition

Where the image sits on the page #composition

Six composition patterns. Each is a relationship between image and text. The system stays the same; the dynamic changes.

A — text · image

Tell the story first.

The image supports the headline. Reading flows naturally left → right.

Default feature row. Alternate sides on consecutive blocks to add rhythm.
B — full bleed

Let the image lead.

For heroes and section breaks. Always add a scrim — text on photo without one is fragile.
Mosaic grid — one anchor + smaller satellites. For about pages and brand showcases.
C — single anchor Image-led. Caption optional.
When the photograph is the whole point. Editorial, case studies, hero sections.
Polaroid stack — playful, biographical. About pages, "the team" sections. Use sparingly.
D — bleed-through

Image behind, text in front.

The photo fades into the surface, headline lives on the clean side.

Masked-bleed composition. The image is felt more than seen. Reserve for opening sections.
05 — Stock vs custom

When each works #stock

Custom photography is always better — and almost never in scope. The honest question is: how do you make stock not look like stock? Treatment, crop, and curation. The bar is "could this have been on the shoot?"

Custom

Earn it for brand moments.

  • Founder portraits, team pages, real customer interviews
  • The hero on the homepage (if the brand is people-led)
  • One signature shot per campaign — better than ten generic ones
  • Half-day shoot covers most needs; don't overscope
Stock

OK for everything else, with rules.

  • Pick from one curated library (Unsplash+, Stocksy, Death to Stock)
  • Apply the same treatment across all — grade, duotone, or desat-pop
  • Avoid the top-200 most-downloaded shots; they're everywhere already
  • Crop aggressively — full-frame stock is the giveaway
  • Never use stock people on a page that talks about your real users
The rescue treatment

Same stock source, two passes. Left is what comes out of the library. Right is what ships on the site.

Apply the same treatment to every photo on the site. Library variety + system unifier = site-as-one-shoot.
06 — Placeholders

What fills the space while it loads #placeholders

Four progressive techniques, in order of increasing investment. Pick one and apply everywhere — the layout shouldn't flicker between strategies as the page loads.

Skeleton0kb
Dominant color4 bytes
Blurhash · LQIP~30 bytes
Loaded~80kb
Skeletonshimmer rect
Use in product UI where the layout matters more than the content (lists, dashboards, profile cards).
Don't use for marketing hero images — the shimmer reads as "loading" not "designed."
Dominant colorsingle average hex
Use for grids and feeds — the color makes the page feel composed before any image lands.
Don't use pure gray. The whole point is anticipating the photo's mood.
Blurhash · LQIPtiny encoded preview
Use for hero, feature, and any photo above the fold. Generated at build time, served with the markup.
Don't rely on the browser blur — it's heavier than the LQIP. Use a real Blurhash.
Native lazyloading="lazy" decoding="async"
Always on every img below the fold. Reduces LCP. Free.
Don't lazy-load the hero — you'll tank LCP. Eager + fetchpriority="high".
07 — The non-negotiables

Imagery rules #rules

One subject category dominates~70% of photos
Set a primary in the kickoff doc. Secondary supports — never two primaries.
Don't rotate through all five categories looking for balance.
One treatment, across the whole sitegrade · duotone · desat-pop
Apply the chosen recipe to every photo at export time. Document it as part of the brand guide.
Don't let "natural" be the default — that's not a choice.
Aspect ratios live in the system--aspect-hero · --aspect-card
Token-ize the ratios. The CMS, the photographer, and the engineer all read from one list.
Don't let editors choose crops in the CMS. Crop once at upload, store all sizes.
Every image has a placeholderblurhash · domcolor · skeleton
Render a placeholder before the image lands. The page should be "designed" at every step of load.
Don't ship an empty rectangle. Empty rectangles are the loudest signal of a half-built site.
Real over genericalways
Always push for real customers, real products, real environments — even at lower fidelity.
Don't use stock for anything the site claims is real. That's the only inviolable rule.

Imagery is the brand register the system can't enforce.

Two sites with identical components look like different companies because of what's in the photos. Pick the dials in this chapter deliberately or the site will feel templated no matter how good the type pairing is.
