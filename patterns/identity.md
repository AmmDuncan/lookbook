# Pattern: Identity — brand → token system + design DNA

**Inherits:** all of `fundamentals.md` (esp. F1, F7, F15, F17, F20, F21, F26, F35–F39, F66). This is the seam **`patterns/approach.md` hands three dials to** (P-AP-05/06): Approach *picks* which brand-archetype column and names the dials; Identity is *how you derive the concrete token values* from a brand input.
**Cite as:** `P-ID-<nn>`.

> **Read this when you have a brand input and need a `:root` skin.** Input is small — a logo, a brand color, a voice, or **one adjective** ("calm" / "bold" / "editorial" / "technical"). Output is a coherent **token override block** (the `packages/tokens/skins/*.css` shape — accent ramp, type pairing, neutral temperature, radius/density/shadow/border dials, motion register) plus the restraint rules that keep it from sliding into decoration. **The spine of this file: applying identity produces TOKENS + RESTRAINT, never ornaments.** A product feels like itself because its *system* is tuned — not because motifs are sprinkled on top. The cautionary precedent is load-bearing: an earlier "signature flourish catalog" drove a clean dashboard to add decorative KPI icons and double-encoded status and looked measurably *worse* (AP20 scope · AP23 · AP24). Identity must not repeat that. Distinctiveness comes from the dials; functional surfaces stay restrained regardless of how bold the brand is.

## Surface intent
Identity is **not a surface** — it's a derivation run once per project (or once when a new brand lands), producing the skin file the whole system references. It sits *below* Approach (Approach decides the column and the one wildcard dial; Identity computes the values) and *above* every pattern (which consume the resulting tokens, never raw hex). The acid test for every rule here: **does it produce distinctiveness through the SYSTEM (token choices, type, spacing, color discipline) rather than through DECORATION?** If a rule could be read as "add a motif/icon/ornament to stand out," it is wrong — cut or reframe it.

## The output shape
A `:root` / `[data-theme="light"]` override block overriding only re-skin dials: `--accent` + `--accent-hover` + `--accent-subtle` + `--focus-ring`, the neutral roles (`--bg`/`--surface`/`--border`/`--text-*`), optionally `--accent-secondary`, the type families (`--font-sans` / `--font-display`), and the personality dials (`--radius-*`, shadow tokens, density via spacing/size-step choice). The scale, weights, tracking, and component structure are **structural constants and never change** (see tokens.css header). Re-skin overrides values; it never adds tokens or hardcodes hex in components.

## Calibrations

**P-ID-01. Identity is a derivation that outputs TOKENS, not a motif. From the brand input, derive a `:root` override skin (accent ramp, neutral temperature, type pairing, radius/density/shadow dials, motion register) — the same shape as `packages/tokens/skins/*.css`. The product becomes recognizably itself through these token values, never through a decorative signature applied across the UI.**
New (the file's spine; SKILL three-layer model layer 2; distills gallery Identity). *Why:* a brand input is a *register*, and a register is expressed by tuning the system, not by bolting on ornaments. Real proof: a settings/billing panel from five products (navy-tight Monarch, yellow-round Sprig, mono-dense Graphite, electric Framer, indigo Lovable) reads as five different companies purely from accent/type/density/radius — none adds a motif to a row or a glyph to a tile. The dropped "signature catalog" failed exactly because it tried to manufacture identity through decoration on a functional surface; the dials are where it actually lives.
*Check: is the deliverable a `:root` token override (accent + neutrals + type + radius/density/shadow), and does identity express through those values rather than any repeated decorative element?*

**P-ID-02. Derive the accent ramp from the brand color AND verify a white-text-capable dark step (F66). The brand hue rarely lands at the right lightness for a button fill: map it to the OKLCH ramp (50→950), pick the fill step (~500/600) for the accent role, and confirm a 600/700-equivalent tonal step carries white text at ≥4.5:1 — OR if the brand hue is intrinsically light (yellow, lime, cyan), use DARK INK on the light accent instead of white. Never assume `--text-on-accent: #fff` works on the raw brand color.**
New (operationalizes F66/F54; the most-shipped contrast bug). *Why:* a brand color is chosen for presence in a fill, not for carrying type — a mid-tone accent gives white text ~3:1. Both wild branches are observable: Monarch's navy *is* the white-text step; Sprig's butter-yellow forces dark ink on the "Buy Now" button. The ramp needs a step dark enough for white, or the control flips to dark-on-light. This is a hard gate, not a guideline — run it.
*Check: does `--accent` (fill) pair with a label color passing ≥4.5:1 — a dark tonal step under white text, or dark ink on a light accent — verified with `node scripts/contrast.mjs "<label>:<fill>:accent-fill"`?*

**P-ID-03. One accent, plus at most one bounded secondary. The skin defines a single accent ramp; a secondary accent (`--accent-secondary` / the gradient) is optional and BOUNDED to hero / CTA / expressive surfaces only — never on cards, atoms, status, or in-product chrome (F17). A brand with a multi-color logo collapses to ONE UI accent; the other brand colors do not become UI colors.**
New (ties F17, F19; the `--accent-secondary` token contract from SKILL/tokens.css). *Why:* accent earns attention by scarcity (F17) — a multi-hue brand palette transplanted into the UI is N accent violations and reads as a costume. Framer proves the discipline: its violet secondary appears *only* on the one Pro pricing tier, never on the settings chrome. Status hues stay reserved for status (F19); brand colors that aren't the accent live in marketing imagery, not UI tokens.
*Check: exactly one accent ramp, ≤1 secondary bounded to expressive placements, and no brand-logo colors leaking into cards/status/chrome as UI accents?*

**P-ID-04. A display face is optional and per-register, not a default "add character" move (F1). Warrant a second (display) family only when the register is editorial / luxe / expressive-marketing and the surface is expressive; otherwise ship ONE workhorse sans and earn typographic character through the dials already in the scale — tracking (F7), a weight jump, tabular/feature numerals, optical sizing. Never more than two families on a screen.**
New (operationalizes F1, F7, AP10). *Why:* "add a display font for personality" is a reflex that often produces AP10's untreated-pair tell or a two-collage. Graphite, Zoom, Monarch read as fully themselves on a single sans — their character is density, accent, and numeral treatment. The display face is a real lever for editorial/luxe brands (a serif headline, a thin display), but it's a *register* decision, not a distinctiveness requirement. When in doubt, one sans treated well beats two families.
*Check: does the project ship one workhorse sans by default, adding a display family ONLY for an editorial/luxe/expressive register — and is character on a sans-only project carried by tracking / weight / numerals, not a second font?*

**P-ID-05. Density, radius, shadow, and border-weight are the four PERSONALITY DIALS — set them as a coherent group from the brand's one adjective. They move together: a register reads as itself only when all four agree. Tight + sharp (radius 0–4) + flat (shadow-sm or none) + hairline borders = technical / fintech / precise. Airy + rounded (radius 12–16) + soft (shadow-md/lg) + minimal borders = friendly / consumer / approachable. Mixing them (rounded + flat + tight) reads as incoherent, not distinctive.**
New (distills the dial group onto tokens.css `--radius-*` / shadow tokens / spacing; ties F26, F21). *Why:* these four are the tactile half of identity and they're correlated in every real product — Monarch is tight+sharp+flat+hairline (precise fintech), Sprig is airy+rounded+soft (friendly tool), Stripe/Linear/Vercel are dense+sharp+near-flat (technical). The brand adjective picks the group: "calm/precise" → tight register, "friendly/warm" → airy register, "editorial" → airy + low radius + restrained shadow. Set them as a set, then let the job's density override per surface (P-ID-06).
*Check: are radius / shadow / border-weight / base-density chosen as one coherent group matching the brand adjective — not mixed across registers?*

**P-ID-06. The job's density overrides the brand's density per surface (F26 wins). The brand sets a *default* density (P-ID-05), but a scan-first surface stays compact and an expressive surface stays sparse regardless of brand. A "friendly airy" consumer brand building a data table still ships a compact table; a "precise tight" fintech brand building a marketing hero still ships sparse. Radius/shadow/accent carry the brand across both; density follows the work.**
New (reconciles P-ID-05 with F26 / P-AP-03). *Why:* density is the #1 generic-AI tell when it comes from the brand instead of the work (F26, P-AP-03) — a dashboard with marketing whitespace. Identity's tactile dials (radius/shadow/accent/type) are what stay constant and make the airy brand's dense table still feel like *that* brand; the density itself bends to the surface. This is the seam that keeps P-ID-05 from fighting F26.
*Check: does the brand set a default density that the per-surface job can override (compact for scan-first, sparse for expressive), with radius/shadow/accent/type carrying identity across both?*

**P-ID-07. Motion character is a dial derived from the same adjective — set the register, defer recipes to `patterns/motion.md`. Map the adjective to a motion register: calm/precise → crisp ~120–200ms ease-standard, no spring; friendly/playful → soft springs + a list stagger; luxe → slow ≥320ms decelerate fades; brutalist → snap, no easing. This picks the `--duration-*` / `--ease-*` defaults the surface reaches for; the per-element timings and the gating-by-density live in motion.md (P-MT-*), and the one signature motion is P-MT-09.**
New (cross-ref `patterns/motion.md`; ties tokens.css `--ease-spring` / `--duration-*`). *Why:* motion has a personality the same way radius does, and it's derived from the same brand adjective so it agrees with the tactile dials (a tight technical product with bouncy springs reads incoherent). But motion.md already owns the recipes and the density gate — Identity only sets the *register* and hands off, so the two files don't duplicate.
*Check: is the motion register (duration band + easing family) derived from the brand adjective and consistent with the tactile dials — with recipes and density-gating left to motion.md?*

**P-ID-08. One signature, on EXPRESSIVE surfaces only, removable. If the project warrants a signature move (marketing hero, empty state, onboarding, 404 — the part-expressive elements), pick exactly ONE signature move (per `personality.template.md`), apply it ≤3×, build it last, and ensure the layout works with it stripped. This is the SAME signature governed by `personality.template.md` and P-AP-07 — Identity does not introduce a second one. On scan-first product surfaces it does not apply at all (P-ID-09).**
New (cross-ref `personality.template.md`, P-AP-07, AP20). *Why:* this is the one place identity touches composition, and it's strictly bounded — a signature is an *expressive-surface* device (Pitch's removable gradient hairline on the billing summary card is the textbook case; Framer's violet tier). Two signatures cancel; a signature on a functional surface is noise (P-ID-09). Identity points at the existing signature machinery rather than owning it.
*Check: at most one signature, only on expressive surfaces, ≤3×, built last, removable — and is it the same single signature P-AP-07 / personality.md govern, not a second?*

**P-ID-09. GUARDRAIL — the scan-first BODY of a functional surface gets ZERO decorative identity; the ONE expressive element on that surface (if it has one) may carry a single restrained, removable brand moment. On the scan-first body — data-table rows, the KPI strip, settings rows, list items, form fields — identity shows ONLY through token discipline: type family, spacing/density, accent spent scarcely (≤5×, F17), neutral temperature, radius. No motifs, no per-card icons, no decorative status double-encoding, no brand-pattern overlays there. BUT a functional surface often has one genuinely expressive element — a current-plan / hero card, a page hero, a showcase tile — and THAT one element may carry a single brand moment (a brand-color field, a gated texture per F36, the one signature of P-ID-08), provided the moment is restrained and removable and the body around it stays zero-decoration. A pure scan-first surface with NO expressive element (a warehouse dashboard, a bare data table) gets zero, full stop — a bold brand and a calm brand produce the SAME restrained table, distinguished only by tokens.**
New (the cautionary-precedent guardrail, scoped; restates AP20-scope · AP23 · AP24 · P-AP-07/08). *Why:* the dropped-Variation failure was decorative identity on the scan-first BODY — KPI-tile icons (AP24) and a double-encoded status channel (AP23) on a dashboard with no hero — and that stays forbidden. But blanket-stripping ALL brand expression from a functional surface over-corrects: a billing panel's current-plan hero card or a settings page's showcase header is a legitimate, gated place for ONE brand moment (the same expressive-element logic as P-ID-08), and killing it makes the product blander without making it better (proven — the no-carve-out version of this rule drove a Pulse billing panel measurably *less* distinctive than the version that kept one coral hero moment, while the body stayed equally restrained either way). The line: scan-first body = zero; the single expressive element on it = at most one restrained, removable moment; no expressive element present = zero.
*Check: is the scan-first BODY free of decorative motif / per-card icon / double-encoded status / brand overlay — AND is brand expression limited to at most ONE restrained, removable moment on the single expressive element (hero / showcase), with zero on a surface that has no expressive element?*

## Composition defaults
- **Run order:** receive brand input (color / voice / one adjective) → derive accent ramp + verify on-color step (P-ID-02) → fix one accent + optional bounded secondary (P-ID-03) → decide type pairing from the curated by-voice menu (`patterns/typefaces.md`, P-TY; specimen `apps/typefaces/index.html`), display face only if the register warrants (P-ID-04) → set the four tactile dials as a coherent group from the adjective (P-ID-05) → set motion register, defer to motion.md (P-ID-07) → write the `:root` skin → per-surface, let the job's density override (P-ID-06) → on expressive surfaces only, optionally one signature (P-ID-08) → on a functional surface, token discipline in the scan-first body + at most one restrained, removable brand moment on its single expressive element (P-ID-09).
- **Adjective → dial starting points** (refine within; the brand-archetype columns live in P-AP-05): *calm/precise/technical* → cool true-neutral, tight density, radius 2–4, flat shadow, hairline border, crisp motion · *friendly/warm/consumer* → warm neutral, airy density, radius 12–16, soft shadow, minimal border, spring motion · *editorial* → near-neutral, airy, low radius, restrained shadow, optional serif display, slow fades · *luxe* → single jewel accent, hero-airy, thin display, slow decelerate · *bold* → saturated accent (still ≤5× on UI), confident type scale, otherwise follows the surface.
- **Two outputs are non-negotiable:** a `:root` skin whose `--accent` passes F66 (run contrast.mjs), and the explicit restraint note that a functional surface's scan-first body carries identity through tokens only, with at most one restrained brand moment on its expressive element (P-ID-09).

## Forbidden moves
- **Manufacturing identity with motifs / per-card icons / decorative overlays** instead of tuning tokens — the dropped-Variation failure (P-ID-01, P-ID-09).
- **Assuming `--text-on-accent: #fff` works on the raw brand color** without deriving a dark tonal step or flipping to dark ink — the most-shipped contrast bug (P-ID-02, F66).
- **Transplanting a multi-color brand palette into UI tokens** — collapse to one accent + at most one bounded secondary (P-ID-03, F17).
- **Adding a display face as a default "character" move** — it's per-register and optional (P-ID-04, F1, AP10).
- **Mixing tactile dials across registers** (rounded + flat + tight) — set them as one coherent group (P-ID-05).
- **Letting the brand set density on a scan-first surface** — the job's density wins (P-ID-06, F26).
- **A bouncy spring on a precise/technical brand** — motion register must agree with the tactile dials (P-ID-07).
- **Two signatures, a signature on a functional surface, or a non-removable one** — exactly one, expressive-only, removable (P-ID-08, P-AP-07).
- **Decorative identity on the scan-first BODY** — per-card icons, double-encoded status, motifs, brand overlays on data-table rows / KPI strip / settings rows; OR more than one brand moment, a non-removable one, or any moment on a surface with no expressive element (P-ID-09, AP20-scope, AP23, AP24).
