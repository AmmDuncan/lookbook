Composed Recipe · Reports / export builder · one skin, five compositions

Same skin. Five ways to build a report.

One job — configure, preview, and export a revenue report — composed five ways. Every frame wears the same neutral skin: off-white page, white cards with hairline borders, slightly-rounded corners, one restrained blue accent, system sans with tabular figures. What changes is purely composition and interaction. The skin is the paint, the composition is the skeleton.

Composition 1
Builder + live preview

PULLS — left config form (date range, dimensions, metrics, group-by) + right live preview of the resulting report table that reflects the config.  WHY — the classic report-builder split: changes apply instantly so users tune the query against real output instead of guessing, then export with confidence.

Composition 2
Pivot + grouped totals

PULLS — a report output table grouped by a dimension with subtotal rows per group + a grand-total row + a toolbar with a group-by control and export buttons (CSV / PDF / XLSX). Tabular, right-aligned numerals.  WHY — for financial reporting that gets read and exported: subtotals and a grand total let the numbers reconcile at a glance, the way a spreadsheet would.

Composition 3
Stepped builder (wizard)

PULLS — a horizontal stepper (Source → Filters → Columns → Format) with the current step's panel shown + a running summary of choices on the side.  WHY — for first-time or complex exports: breaking the build into ordered steps lowers the chance of a misconfigured report, and the summary keeps earlier decisions visible.

Composition 4
Saved reports library

PULLS — a list of saved report definitions (name + last-run + owner + schedule badge) with run/preview actions, plus a primary “New report” CTA.  WHY — once reports are reusable, the home becomes a library: people re-run known reports far more often than they build new ones, so running and scheduling come first.

Scheduled exports

Skins & how to apply them

A skin is the full set of visual tokens — color, type, radius, elevation, and density — applied uniformly. Pick ONE skin per product and apply it across whichever compositions you choose: the composition is the skeleton, the skin is the paint, so any composition above renders coherently in any skin. Every frame on this page uses the Neutral column.
