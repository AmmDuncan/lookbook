Composed Recipe · Admin / user management · one skin, five compositions

Same skin. Five ways to run a team.

One workspace's people — members, roles, invites, and seats — composed five ways. Every frame wears the same neutral skin: off-white page, white surfaces with hairline borders, slightly-rounded corners, one restrained blue accent, system sans with tabular figures. What changes is purely composition and interaction. The skin is the paint, the composition is the skeleton.

Composition 1
Members table + roles

PULLS — a members table (avatar, name, email, role dropdown, status badge, last active) + a toolbar with search and an Invite button.  WHY — the everyday admin view: an inline role dropdown makes the most common change one click, and a status column surfaces who's active, invited, or suspended at a glance.

Composition 2
Roles & permissions matrix

PULLS — a matrix with roles as columns, capability rows, and toggles at each intersection (with a visible sliding thumb).  WHY — for fine-grained access control: a grid makes the whole policy legible at once, so you can see exactly what each role can do and tune it in place.

Composition 3
Invite flow + pending

PULLS — an invite form (email + role select) + a list of pending invites with Resend / Revoke actions.  WHY — onboarding is its own task: pairing the send form with the outstanding queue keeps “who haven't I heard back from” visible right where you invite.

Composition 4
Member detail drawer

PULLS — a members list + a right drawer for the selected member (role, teams, recent activity, and a danger-zone Remove).  WHY — managing one person needs more than a row holds; a drawer opens their full context — and the irreversible action — without leaving the list.

Apply
Skins & how to apply them

A skin is the full set of visual tokens — color, type, radius, elevation, and density — applied uniformly. Pick ONE skin per product and apply it across whichever compositions you choose: the composition is the skeleton, the skin is the paint, so any composition above renders coherently in any skin. Every frame on this page uses the Neutral column.
