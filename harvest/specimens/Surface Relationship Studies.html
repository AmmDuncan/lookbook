Surface relationship studies

Eight ways the nav surface can relate to the content surface. Nav items, page content and skin are identical in every frame — the only variable is the relationship between the two planes. This is the axis that makes a set of "distinct" shells stop reading as one design drawn eight times. Four further axes follow: the same relations on a horizontal split, where the header sits, which plane owns the scroll, and what collapse does to all of it.

Why this axis exists. Most shell libraries vary nav content — icon rail, nested groups, workspace switcher — while every specimen separates nav from content the same way: a 1px border with the sidebar flush to the viewport edge. That is one idea drawn N times.

How to read it. Pick the relation whose job matches, then apply any skin. These are composition, not paint: the greyscale strip at the bottom is the proof — each frame must still read as a distinct structure with all colour removed.

The interior is held constant on purpose. KPI tiles and the table use one treatment in all eight frames, so nothing inside the content plane can be mistaken for the axis. The single exception is 07, where removing the containers is the relation.

Axis A

Nav plane → content plane

Ordered from most contained to least. Each caption names the mechanism doing the separating, because that mechanism is the thing you are choosing.

01Bordered — the baseline
The default nearly every admin ships. Honest and legible, but it is the reason shell sets look alike. Use when the product is dense and any tonal step would compete with data.
separator: 1px rule · nav flush to edge · one ground
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
02Same level — space alone
No rule, no tonal step: nav and content share one ground and a wide gutter does the separating. Calmest of the eight. Use when the nav is short and the page is text-forward.
separator: gutter only · no rule · no tonal step
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
03Sunken nav, raised content
Nav recedes into the page ground; content lifts off it as one white plane. Puts the work visually in front. Use when the nav is large and you need it to stop shouting.
separator: elevation · nav on recess · content raised
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
04Floating content
Content becomes an inset card with a gap on all four sides; the nav is flush to the ground and reads as the application frame. Use when the page is a single focused object.
separator: gap + radius + elevation · content detached
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
05Inset nav
The inverse of 04: the nav is the floating card and the content sits directly on the page ground with no container. Nav reads as a tool, not as structure. Use when content should feel boundless.
separator: gap + radius on the NAV · content uncontained
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
06Ink rail, detached content
Dark chrome flush to the edge with the content plane floating clear of it. Strongest figure/ground split of the eight. Use when the product wants a hard identity edge without a coloured brand band.
separator: tonal inversion + gap · dark frame, light plane
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
07Full bleed — nav is the only chrome
No gutter, no card, no well: rules run edge to edge and the nav is the sole piece of chrome on screen. Highest data density of the eight. Use when the page is a table and every pixel of width earns its keep.
separator: nav tint only · content bleeds to frame edge
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
08Two tone — no rule
Nav on a tinted ground, content pure white, nothing drawn between them. The tonal step alone carries the split, which is quieter than a rule and cheaper than elevation. Use when you want 01's clarity without 01's boxed-in look.
separator: tonal step only · no rule, no gap, no shadow
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
Axis B

Where the header sits relative to the split

A second, independent choice: the header can span both planes, sit over the content plane only, or not exist at all as chrome. It changes what the nav is understood to belong to — the app, or the page.

B1Header spans both planes
Brand and global actions sit above everything; the nav is subordinate to the header. Reads as: one application, many sections. Costs: vertical space on every page.
header full width · nav starts below it
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
B2Header over content only
Nav owns the full height and holds the brand; the header belongs to the page, not the app. Reads as: the nav is the product. Costs: global actions have to live in the nav or the page header.
nav full height · header scoped to the content plane
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
B3No header — the page owns its title
No chrome bar at all: the content plane opens with its own title block. Reads as: a document. Costs: nowhere persistent for search, notifications or environment badges.
zero header chrome · title block inside the content plane
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
A×BCombining the two axes
Axis A and Axis B are independent — any relation takes any header position. These are the pairings worth reaching for first.
01 bordered + B1 spanningThe safe government/enterprise default. Nothing to explain, nothing to admire.
04 floating + B2 over contentThe header rides on the floating plane, so the card reads as a whole document.
06 ink rail + B2 over contentStrongest identity split. The dark rail carries brand, the light plane carries work.
07 full bleed + B1 spanningMaximum data density: one chrome bar, one nav, everything else is table.
05 inset nav + B1 spanningAvoid. A full-width header above a floating nav card leaves the nav orphaned between two grounds.
Axis C

The same relations on a horizontal split

Swap the sidebar for a top bar and the eight relations still apply — but they stop reading the same way. A bar has far less surface area than a rail, so tone and elevation do more work and the gutter does less. Six shown; 07 full-bleed and 08 two-tone transfer unchanged and are not repeated. Note the bar carries five destinations where the rail carried seven — a horizontal nav holds fewer items before it truncates, and pushing the remainder into an overflow menu is part of the trade, not an implementation detail.

C1Bordered bar
The horizontal baseline, and the same trap as 01: a rule under the bar is what every marketing-site-turned-app reaches for first.
separator: 1px rule under a full-width bar
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
C2Same level
No rule. On a horizontal split this is riskier than 02 was: with only 38px of bar, losing the rule can leave the nav floating in the page rather than framing it.
separator: space alone · no rule, no tonal step
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
C3Sunken bar, raised content
The bar recedes into the ground and the page lifts in front of it. Works better horizontally than vertically — the eye reads the raised edge as the top of a document.
separator: elevation · bar on recess, content raised
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
C4Floating content
Content as an inset card below a flush bar. The strongest "this page is one object" reading available on a horizontal split.
separator: gap + radius + elevation below the bar
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
C5Inset bar
The bar becomes a floating card and the content sits on the ground — the horizontal twin of 05. Reads as a toolbar hovering over a workspace.
separator: gap + radius on the BAR · content uncontained
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
C6Ink bar, detached content
Dark bar, light plane floating below it. Carries brand in the smallest area of any relation here, which is why so many products land on it.
separator: tonal inversion + gap

Axis D

Which plane owns the scroll

Independent of every relation above, and the one most often decided by accident. The scrollbars in these four are live — the table is lengthened here so the overflow is real rather than drawn. Each frame is parked mid-scroll so the difference reads in a still; scroll inside one to feel it.

D1Whole page scrolls
One scroll container: the nav travels up and off with the content. Costs: nav is unreachable deep in a long list. Use when the page is a document, not a workspace.
one scroll container · nav scrolls away
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
D2Nav fixed, content scrolls
The app default. Nav is always reachable; only the work moves. Costs: a nav longer than the viewport becomes unreachable, which is what D3 fixes.
nav fixed · one scroll container in the content plane
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
D3Both scroll independently
Two scroll containers side by side. Use when the nav is genuinely long (workspaces, projects, saved views). Costs: two scrollbars is a real cognitive tax — don't reach for it early.
two scroll containers · nav lengthened here so its overflow is real
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
D4Content scrolls under a pinned header
D2 plus a sticky page header, so the title and primary action survive the scroll. Use when the page action stays relevant at row 200 — which on a registrations table it does.
nav fixed · page header sticky INSIDE the scroll container
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
Axis E

Collapse is a relation change, not a width change

All four hold relation 04 (floating content) constant and vary only the nav. Treating collapse as "the same nav, narrower" is the mistake: at 52px the nav stops reading as structure and starts reading as a toolbar, and the content plane silently becomes the whole product.

E1Open
Labels visible, sections named. The content plane reads as one page inside an application.
150px · labels + section headings
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
E2Icon rail
Same relation, but the balance flips: the plane now dominates and the nav reads as a toolbar. Costs: section headings are gone, so grouping has to survive in icon order alone.
52px · icons only · labels via tooltip
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
E3Rail + hover flyout
The labels come back on demand, floating over the content plane — which is a third surface, and needs its own elevation step. Use when you want E2's density without losing wayfinding.
52px rail · expanded panel overlays the plane · covers the page title while open
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
E4Off canvas
No nav at rest; a control in the page header summons it. The relation has collapsed to nothing and the plane is the product. Costs: zero persistent wayfinding — every user is one click from lost.
0px · nav summoned · content plane full width
dvla.gov.gh/registrations
Proof

The same eight, unskinned

Colour removed. If a relation stops being distinguishable here, it was skin, not composition — and it does not belong in the set. All eight survive; 01, 02 and 08 sit closest together, which is exactly the trade each of them is making.

Lifting from this sheet: copy the frame's markup once, then copy only the .rN / .cN / .dN / .eN block — every variant is 3–8 declarations over the same shared interior. The interior classes (.nav, .main, .kpi, .tbl) are deliberately relation-agnostic so switching relation is a one-class change, not a rebuild.
