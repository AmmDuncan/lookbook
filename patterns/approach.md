# Pattern: Approach — the opening move

**Inherits:** all of `fundamentals.md`. This is the layer **above** the system — the strategy you run *before* composing a single pixel, so the system gets pointed in the right direction.
**Cite as:** `P-AP-<nn>`.

> **Read this first, for strategy.** Every other pattern file answers *how to compose a surface*; this one answers *what to compose and why* — the decisions that come before the page-composition protocol in `SKILL.md`. The cardinal rule there is **converge on vocabulary, diverge on composition**; Approach is what tells you *which* archetype, *which* density, *which* dial values, and *which* single signature you'll then diverge with. Get this layer right and the "2–3 directions" step produces three genuinely-good options; get it wrong and you generate three polished answers to the wrong question. Weak approach is the upstream cause of the two worst tells: **AP20** (defensive, forgettable — no opinion was ever formed) and **AP22** (tells-instead-of-shows — the design describes because no one decided what it should *show*).

## Surface intent
Approach is not a surface — it's a **decision framework applied once per project, once per new flow, or once per redesign**, producing a short, written readout (the kickoff one-pager, P-AP-09) that the rest of the work is held against. It **begins by asking the user the framing inputs** (the Frame-it intake, P-AP-11) — never by guessing them — and applies to all three situations from `SKILL.md`: a greenfield site (full brief), a new flow inside an established app (compressed brief — P-AP-02), and a redesign of an existing surface (the intake's redesign branch + the four-lens audit, P-AP-08). The output is a set of *committed choices* — job, archetype, density, tone, the six dial values, the one signature — not a mood board. Because it's a decision framework, the rules below favor **decision-prompts and contextual branches over rigid mandates**: they tell you *what to decide and how the inputs map to the choice*, not *what the answer must be*. The answer is the project's; the discipline is making the choice deliberately and writing it down.

## Density band
Approach **sets** the density the rest of the work expresses (F26) — it doesn't have a density of its own. The single most important output of P-AP-03 is the density decision, because wrong density is the #1 generic-AI tell (F26): a dashboard with marketing whitespace, or a marketing page with dashboard compression. Everything downstream (texture gating F36, motion gating `patterns/motion.md`, the pattern file's band) inherits from the density Approach picks.

## Calibrations

**P-AP-01. State the job-to-be-done in ONE sentence before anything else: who shows up, what they're trying to do, and what "success" looks like in the first 10 seconds.** "A solo developer comparing analytics pricing on a Wednesday wants to know if this fits their stack and what it costs." If you can't write that sentence, you are not ready to compose — every downstream rank/emphasis/density decision will be a coin-flip.
New (distills gallery Approach §01 Q1–Q2). *Why:* the job is the load-bearing input — it drives the archetype, the density, the primary action, and what the page must *show*. Real products prove it: Monzo's job ("see my money + recent activity in 2 seconds") produces a bold balance card over a calm transaction list; Figma's job ("maximize the canvas, make chrome disappear") produces a near-mono dense 3-panel tool. Same component kit could build either — the *job* is why they look nothing alike.
*Check: can you state the job-to-be-done in one sentence — visitor, intent, 10-second success — before composing?*

**P-AP-02. Match the discovery depth to the engagement, don't run agency ceremony on a feature. New marketing site / brand → the full brief (gallery's 12 questions: visitor, primary action + metric, brand maturity, 3 liked refs + 1 disliked + why, audience tone, existing imagery, pages in/out of scope, decider/gatekeeper, deadline, who-builds-it, the embarrassment guardrail). A new flow inside an established app → the compressed brief: job (P-AP-01), the one primary action, which archetype, and the app's existing dials (inherit, don't re-derive).**
New (distills gallery Approach §01; calibrated — see evidence). *Why:* the gallery's "one 90-minute call, these 12 questions in order" is right for client/agency work and over-heavy for an in-app feature where the dials are already set. The discipline that survives at every scale is small: *name the job, name the one primary action, name the archetype, know the guardrail*. Don't skip it (that's how flows ship without an opinion — AP20); don't inflate it into a ritual that delays the work.
*Check: for the engagement size, did you run the right-weight brief — full for greenfield, compressed for an in-app flow — and write down the job + primary action + archetype + guardrail?*

**P-AP-03. Derive density and archetype from the job + content shape, NOT from the brand. Ask: is the user *scanning data to act* (compact, scan-first) or *reading/being-persuaded* (sparse, expressive) or *filling/editing* (medium)? That answer picks the archetype and locks the density before any personality is applied.**
New (extends gallery §03; ties F26). *Why:* density and archetype come from the *work*, brand comes *after* (P-AP-05). A fintech dashboard and a fintech marketing page share a brand but not a density — conflating "what brand" with "what density" is exactly the F26 tell. The content shape decides: a long/growing list → CRUD-list (compact); a record → detail (medium hero + scan-first body); a KPI-and-charts surface → dashboard (compact, near-mono); a persuade-and-convert page → marketing (sparse, expressive). Battle-tested across domains: Figma/Replit (scan/act → maximally compact, near-mono), Retro (enjoy content → comfortable, imagery-led).
*Check: did density and archetype fall out of the job + content shape (scan-to-act / read-to-decide / fill-to-complete) — and is that density the one F26 expects for the surface?*

**P-AP-04. Name the ONE primary action per surface and the metric it moves; everything else is subordinate. Two co-equal primaries means you haven't decided.** Sign-up, demo, deposit, "save & continue", "view all transactions" — one verb, named (F68), measured. A pricing CTA sitting next to a careers link at equal weight is a failure of approach, not of layout.
New (distills gallery §01 Q2, §06; ties F16, AP18). *Why:* the primary action is the spine the composition hangs on — it sets the focal region, the accent's single most important placement (F17), and the emphasis you'll vary across the 2–3 directions. Deciding it at approach-time is what prevents the downstream AP18 (two equal buttons) and F16 (no primary) failures — those are symptoms of a primary never chosen up front.
*Check: one named, verb-labelled primary action per surface, tied to a success metric — not two co-equal CTAs?*

**P-AP-05. Set the six personality dials from the brand: pick the closest brand-archetype column, take FIVE dials from it, and choose exactly ONE wildcard from another column. The six dials are: accent + intensity, type pairing, radius personality, density default, motion vocabulary, texture appetite.** Brand archetype columns (starting points, refine within): **Editorial** (restrained, serif display, airy, slow fades) · **Technical** (mono accents, high contrast, comfortable, crisp ~120ms, status-only color) · **Soft-consumer** (warm palette, rounded radii, moderate accent, soft springs) · **Brutalist** (no radius, mono-as-display, tight, snap no-easing) · **Luxe** (single jewel accent, thin display, hero-airy, slow ≥320ms) · **Playful** (saturated palette, expressive, rounded, spring + list staggers).
New (distills gallery §03 brand→dials table + "5-of-6" rule; SKILL three-layer model layer 2). *Why:* this is the *personality* layer, applied **on top of** the job-derived density/archetype (P-AP-03) — not instead of it. Five dials in one column make the product feel coherent and on-archetype; the single wildcard is what makes *this* technical product different from the next one. Battle-tested: Monzo = soft-consumer dials + one saturated-accent wildcard (the coral balance card); Replit = technical dials held with strict status-only color. Note: P-AP-03's job-derived density can *override* the column's density default — the job wins (a Luxe brand building a data dashboard is still compact). The two seams this hands off to are **Identity** (how the wordmark yields accent / display font / radius — three of the dials) and the brand-voice axes; do not re-derive those here, set the dials and cross-ref.
*Check: five of six dials in one brand-archetype column, exactly one deliberate wildcard named — and the wildcard is a single dial, not a second costume?*

**P-AP-06. One wildcard, and it's a DIAL — not the signature move. Keep the two separate: the wildcard is one of the six dials borrowed from a different column (e.g. a Technical product taking Playful *motion*); the signature is one composition play (e.g. oversized type, edge-anchored label) — the single move `personality.template.md` / P-AP-07 govern. A project gets at most one of each, and they should not fight.**
New (separates gallery §03 wildcard from the signature move; SKILL three-layer model). *Why:* these are two different levers at two different layers and conflating them produces the "costume problem" — a Technical product with all six Playful dials *and* three signature moves reads as a theme, not a system. The wildcard tunes a *token-level* register (motion feel, radius, accent intensity); the signature is a *composition-level* move built last and removable (P-AP-07). One of each, chosen to reinforce — not cancel — each other.
*Check: is the wildcard a single borrowed dial, and the signature a single composition play, and are they distinct decisions that complement rather than compete?*

**P-AP-07. On expressive surfaces, pick a single signature move at approach-time — but build it LAST and make it removable.** Choose one composition play (SKILL's three-layer model; gallery → **Variation** for the catalog) that suits the archetype + job; commit to it in the readout; apply it ≤3 times; the layout must still work with it stripped out. **On scan-first product surfaces (dashboard, table, settings): no decorative signature at all.** Quality there comes from clarity, density, and a single clean status channel — not a gesture. A flourish bolted onto a functional surface is AP23/AP24 noise, not personality; restraint is the goal (AP20 does not bite calm product UI).
New (distills SKILL three-layer model layer 3; ties AP20, AP22, AP23/24). *Why:* deciding the signature at approach-time is what guarantees an *expressive* design will have an opinion — the pre-commitment that defeats AP20 (forgettable) and AP22 (all-tell-no-show). Building it last and keeping it removable is what keeps it from becoming load-bearing or multiplying into a costume. Two signatures cancel out; zero on an expressive surface is the AP20 tell. **But the inverse is the trap on functional surfaces:** a dashboard does not get a "one allowed flourish" — it gets none, because there the signature *is* the noise (the battle-tested warehouse regression: a status edge-marker + KPI icons made a clean dashboard worse). Variety on functional surfaces comes from composition variants (P-AP-10), never decoration.
*Check: on expressive surfaces, exactly one signature named in the readout, ≤3×, removable; on scan-first surfaces, zero decorative signature — functional treatments only?*

**P-AP-08. Audit before you redesign an inherited surface — four lenses: information architecture, content, brand, performance. Decide what to keep, fix, and cut before generating anything new.** IA (sitemap on one page, ≤7 nav items, every page has one 5-word job, no orphans) · content (hero says what it does in ≤8 words, benefits not features, sentence case, verbs for CTAs, real names/data not placeholders) · brand (one consistent accent, type pair holds, one icon family + stroke, made-not-generic empty/404 states) · performance (LCP <2.5s, hero <200kb AVIF/WebP, fonts <100kb, no CLS, keyboard nav, AA contrast, reduced-motion intact).
New (distills gallery Approach §02). *Why:* redesigning without auditing throws away the parts that worked and re-introduces the parts that didn't. The audit is also where you discover the *real* job (P-AP-01) — the gap between what the site does and what users came for. On an established app this is the cheapest way to inherit the existing dials (P-AP-02) correctly instead of guessing them.
*Check: for an inherited surface, did you run the four-lens audit and record keep / fix / cut decisions before composing the replacement?*

**P-AP-09. Write the readout — the kickoff one-pager — and hold every later decision against it. One short doc: the visitor, the one primary action + metric, the site/content shape, the archetype + the five-dial column, the one wildcard dial, the one signature, the three liked + one disliked references, the embarrassment guardrail, and the explicit out-of-scope list.** When a reviewer asks for "more colour", point at the wildcard line and ask which dial they want to trade.
New (distills gallery Approach §06). *Why:* the readout is the receipt that makes the approach *auditable* — the same way citing `F<n>` IDs makes the build auditable (SKILL). Without it, the job/dials/signature decisions evaporate by mid-project and the work drifts back to the safe mean (AP20). The single most valuable line is the embarrassment guardrail — it's the one input worth more than the designer's taste, and the fastest way to catch a generic result before it ships ("looking like a Tailwind template; default gradient buttons; stock photos of people pointing at laptops" — which is AP1, AP13, AP15 named in the client's own words).
*Check: is there a one-page readout naming visitor · primary action + metric · shape · archetype + dial column · wildcard · signature · references · embarrassment guardrail · out-of-scope — and does it stay visible for the whole project?*

**P-AP-10. The readout feeds the 2–3 directions, it does not replace them. Approach fixes the *invariants* (job, archetype, density, dials, the one signature); the composition protocol then varies only the *composables* — emphasis (focal region), region split, and section rhythm — to produce 2–3 on-system directions. Never re-decide the dials per direction; never ship one direction without having considered the alternatives.**
New (bridges to SKILL "How to make 3 directions"). *Why:* this is the explicit hand-off. The whole point of nailing the approach is that the three directions are then three *good* arrangements of one well-chosen kit, not three different design languages or three answers to three different briefs. If the directions differ in accent, type, or density, the approach leaked into the composition layer — pull it back. Diverge on composition, converge on everything Approach decided.
*Check: do the 2–3 directions share identical dials/archetype/signature and differ only in emphasis / split / rhythm — with the chosen one traceable back to a readout line?*

**P-AP-11. Run the Frame-it intake — ASK the user the framing inputs through the host's structured question UI before composing; never guess the dials. Adapt the question set to the situation: a NEW product/site, a NEW flow in an existing app, or a REDESIGN.** A short multiple-choice interview (not a prose form), each answer mapping to a downstream decision, then written into the readout (P-AP-09):
- **Core (≤4–5 questions, multiple-choice, lead each with one recommended option):** (1) **personality** — calm/precise · bold/energetic · friendly/warm · editorial · technical → sets the six dials (P-AP-05) and the tactile group radius/shadow/density/motion (`identity.md` P-ID-05); (2) **accent / brand** — *new*: pick a direction or let the system derive one and verify on-color (F66 / P-ID-02); *existing*: keep the current brand or evolve it — never silently re-derive a live brand; (3) **theme** — light · dark · both; (4) **platform priority** — desktop · mobile · both (sets the reflow budget, F50–53 / `mobile.md`); (5) **scope** — which screens/flows are in and out (feeds P-AP-09's out-of-scope line).
- **New product/site:** also brand maturity + three-liked / one-disliked references + the embarrassment guardrail (the full brief, P-AP-02 / P-AP-09).
- **New flow in an existing app:** SKIP personality/accent/theme — inherit the app's already-set dials; ask only the flow's job (P-AP-01), its one primary action, the archetype, and which existing components it reuses.
- **Redesign:** also ask (a) what's not working today (the pain that triggered it), (b) what must stay (sacred cows — brand, a beloved flow, a hard constraint), and (c) how far to go — **reskin** (tokens only) · **restructure** (same content, new composition) · **rebuild** (new IA) — then run the four-lens audit (P-AP-08) before composing.
New (operationalizes P-AP-01/02/03/05 into an actual interview; validated live — a 4-question intake set the dials for a calm-focused todo app that then shipped a uniform 12-screen set, auth → states → settings, desktop + mobile). *Why:* the dials are the project's to choose, not the designer's to assume — guessing them is how a design answers the wrong brief (AP20/AP22) or imposes a personality the user never wanted. Four or five sharp multiple-choice questions cost one round-trip and remove the biggest source of rework. Use the host's structured question UI so the user *picks* rather than writes essays, lead each question with a recommendation (so "just go with sensible defaults" is one click), and adapt the set to the situation so an in-app flow isn't interrogated like a greenfield brand and a redesign surfaces its real constraints. The answers populate the readout (P-AP-09) verbatim. **This intake has a post-present mirror — the Review intake (`SKILL.md`):** after the design is built and shown, pull structured *reaction* the same way (gut-check + which levers to change, incl. "feels flat/boring") and revise on it. The two bookend the work — intake pulls *direction* before, Review pulls *reaction* after — so the result converges on the user's eye, not the builder's self-grade.
*Check: before composing a new surface or a redesign, did you ASK the framing inputs via structured multiple-choice (set adapted to new / in-app-flow / redesign), lead with a recommendation, and write the answers into the readout — rather than guessing the dials?*

## Composition defaults
- **Run order:** Frame-it intake — ask the user (P-AP-11) → job (P-AP-01) → right-weight brief (P-AP-02) → density + archetype from job/content (P-AP-03) → primary action (P-AP-04) → six dials, 5-in-column + 1 wildcard (P-AP-05/06) → one signature, chosen now / built last (P-AP-07) → [audit first if inherited/redesign, P-AP-08] → readout (P-AP-09) → hand to the 2–3 directions protocol (P-AP-10).
- **Invariants vs composables:** Approach fixes the invariants (job, archetype, density, dials, signature); the composition layer varies the composables (emphasis, split, rhythm). Never blur the line.
- **Site shapes (for greenfield, decides nav + which templates to budget):** product-marketing (budget hero + pricing + customer-story first) · product-app (the shell + one canonical CRUD list carries the system) · agency/portfolio (one case-study template used 6–12×) · docs (search + article template + code-block treatment) · editorial (article template + index card + typography). Most real sites are one shape or a hybrid of two.
- **Density is the first and most consequential output** — it gates texture (F36), motion (`patterns/motion.md`), and the pattern band. Decide it from the job, not the brand.
- **Two outputs are non-negotiable:** the one-sentence job (P-AP-01) and the one-page readout (P-AP-09). Everything else can be compressed for small flows; these two cannot.

## Forbidden moves
- **Composing before the job is stated in one sentence** — guarantees the design answers the wrong question (P-AP-01).
- **Running the full 12-question agency brief on a small in-app flow** — ceremony without payoff; use the compressed brief (P-AP-02).
- **Deriving density from the brand instead of the job** — the F26 generic-AI tell at its source (P-AP-03).
- **Two co-equal primary actions** — a primary never decided at approach-time; becomes AP18 / F16 downstream (P-AP-04).
- **Mixing all six dials from different columns** — the costume problem; five in one column, one wildcard (P-AP-05).
- **Conflating the wildcard dial with the signature move** — two levers at two layers; one of each, kept distinct (P-AP-06).
- **Zero signature on an expressive surface (AP20) OR two competing signatures (the costume)** — exactly one, removable, built last (P-AP-07).
- **Redesigning an inherited surface without auditing it** — throws away what worked, re-imports what didn't (P-AP-08).
- **Skipping the readout** — the decisions evaporate and the work drifts to the safe mean (P-AP-09, AP20).
- **Re-deciding the dials/archetype per composition direction** — the approach leaking into the composition layer; directions vary only emphasis/split/rhythm (P-AP-10).
- **Treating Approach as marketing-site-only** — it applies to in-app flows too (Mode B), in compressed form (P-AP-02).
- **Guessing the dials instead of asking** — composing a new surface or a redesign without running the Frame-it intake; imposes a personality/accent/density the user never chose (P-AP-11).
