# Pattern: Pricing

**Inherits:** all of `fundamentals.md`, and the marketing surface (`patterns/marketing.md` — spacious density, show-don't-tell, trust-near-the-decision) unless overridden below.
**Cite as:** `P-PR-<nn>`.

## Surface intent
The single highest-stakes conversion surface — the page where an interested reader becomes a paying decision. The reader has already accepted *that* they might pay; the page's job is to answer **how much**, **which tier is for me**, and **what do I lose by picking the cheaper one** — fast, honestly, and without making the choice feel like a trap. It is a *comparison* surface masquerading as a marketing one: it inherits marketing's spaciousness and trust-at-the-decision, but its core device (a row of tier cards the eye reads *across*, not down) is closer to a small data table than a hero. The failure mode is not ugliness — it's friction: a reader who can't tell the tiers apart, can't find the recommended one, or can't trust the number leaves. Every element earns the click on one CTA.

## Density band
**Spacious (F26)**, inherited from marketing — but the tier-card *row* is the one medium-density island on the page. Section padding 80–128px around the card row (F11); the cards themselves use medium inner padding (24–32px, F27) because they hold a price, a descriptor, and a feature list that must stay scannable. The optional comparison matrix below the cards is the densest region (compact rows, 40–48px). Texture: atmospheric only, at most one, behind the hero band — never on or behind the cards (they're a comparison instrument; F36 medium-band restraint applies to the card row even on a spacious page).

## Calibrations

**P-PR-01. 2–4 tiers in one card row — never more.** Three is the canonical count (a low/recommended/high triplet); four only when the fourth is an Enterprise "contact us" tier (P-PR-09). A fifth tier is a forbidden move — the eye can't compare five things across, and it signals the product hasn't decided who it's for.
New (refines AP5 for this surface). *Why:* every reference page lands at 3 or 4 (Jasper/Sana/HoneyBook/Going = 3; Synthesia/monday/Webflow/Rows = 4, with the 4th always Enterprise). Five+ is the pricing-page version of AP5's "refused to rank" — `evidence/pricing.md`.

**P-PR-02. Each tier card holds, in this order: tier name → price-with-period → one-line who-it's-for → feature list (what THIS tier adds) → ONE CTA.** Optionally a "Most popular" badge above the name on the lifted tier (P-PR-04). Nothing else — no tier-specific illustration, no per-card hero image.
New. *Why:* this is the universal card anatomy across all 15 reference screens. The "who-it's-for" line ("For expert features and small teams" — Jasper; "Save hundreds on economy fares" — Going) is what lets a reader self-select before reading a single feature; dropping it forces them to reverse-engineer the audience from the feature list. Feature lists read as "Everything in Starter, plus:" — each tier shows its *delta*, not the full cumulative list (HoneyBook, Sana, monday, Webflow).

**P-PR-03. The price is the hero of the card — the largest type on the surface after the page H1. 40–64px desktop, weight 600–700, tracking −0.02em (F4/F7).** Period and currency are subordinate: currency symbol and `/mo` at 14–18px muted, set as a baseline-aligned superscript or trailing unit, not the same size as the number. When an annual discount strikes the original price, the struck figure is small + muted + `line-through`; the discounted number is the hero.
Refines F4, F7, F13. *Why:* the number is the answer to the page's main question — it must out-rank everything in the card. HoneyBook's `$15` at ~44px with a superscript `.20` and a struck `$19` above is the textbook treatment; Jasper's `$69*` dwarfs its own feature list. A price set at the same size as the tier name (the timid default) buries the one fact the reader came for.

**P-PR-04. Lift ONE tier — the recommended one — without breaking F16 (one primary) or tripping AP5 (three equal cards). Use a combination of: a "Most popular" badge, a 1–2px accent border or ring, a subtle accent-tint card fill, a slight scale-up (~4–8% taller), AND the only filled CTA. The other tiers get ghost/outline CTAs.** The lift is the editorial point of view the row needs; three identically-weighted cards is the tell.
New (the P-PR answer to AP5). *Why:* AP5 fires when equal cards refuse to say which matters; the recommended-tier lift is exactly that editorial decision made visible. Every reference page that has a recommended tier lifts it by *stacking* cues, not one: Jasper (accent ring + "Pro" badge + only-filled CTA), monday ("Most Popular" badge + accent top-rule + pre-selected radio), Webflow (badge + tinted card + filled CTA), Going (tinted column + only-filled CTA), HoneyBook (badge), Rows ("Popular" pill). The filled CTA on the lifted tier *is* the F16 primary; the others being ghost is what keeps it the only primary.

**P-PR-05. Monthly/annual billing toggle sits ABOVE the card row, centered or right-aligned, with an explicit savings cue attached ("Save 20%", "2 months free", "−25%").** A segmented control or a switch; the saved-money figure is the persuasion, so it's never hidden — it rides right beside the annual option. Toggling updates every card's price in place (F29 motion, ≤250ms). Annual is a defensible default-on; declare it.
New. *Why:* the toggle is the page's one piece of real interactivity and the lever that lifts ARPU — it must be obvious and its payoff named. Deezer ("Annual −25%"), HoneyBook ("20% discount"), Sana ("Save €60/year"), monday ("Yearly SAVE 18%"), Webflow ("Save up to 33%"), Rows ("Annually −25%") all pin the savings cue to the toggle. A toggle with no savings number is a switch with no reason to flip it.

**P-PR-06. Per-tier CTA hierarchy: the recommended tier's CTA is the one filled accent button (F16/F66); every other tier's CTA is ghost/outline.** Enterprise is a ghost "Contact sales" / "Talk to sales". The user's *current* plan renders as a disabled/quiet "Current plan" pill, not a button. Buttons are verbs completing "I want to ___" (F68): "Start free trial", "Choose Growth", "Contact sales" — never "Submit" / "Buy".
Refines F16, F66, F68, P-M-03. *Why:* a row where every CTA is a filled accent button is the pricing face of AP18 (no chosen primary) — the eye has no entry point and the page abdicates its recommendation. Going (Premium filled, Elite ghost), Jasper (Pro filled, rest ghost), Webflow (Growth/Core filled, Starter disabled "You are on Starter", Enterprise ghost) all show the hierarchy. *Contextual nuance:* a page MAY filled-weight two adjacent paid tiers when it genuinely pushes a two-step upgrade ladder (Webflow Core + Growth) — but never all of them, and the recommended one still reads as primary via the badge + lift (P-PR-04). **F66 is load-bearing here:** the filled CTA is white-on-accent — verify the accent has a step dark enough to carry white text (compute it), or use dark ink on a light accent.

**P-PR-07. Feature COMPARISON matrix is contextual — add it BELOW the cards, don't replace them, and only when the tiers differ on many capabilities a feature-snippet list can't honestly carry. Cards-only when each tier's delta is ≤6 lines.** When present: a row per feature grouped under category headers, tier columns aligned to the cards above, presence encoded by a checkmark (✓) and absence by an explicit `—` or muted ✗ — never a blank cell (a blank reads as "unknown", not "no"). Values that aren't boolean (seat counts, limits, "2%") sit in the cell, numeric-aligned (F24). Row labels left-aligned; the matrix shares the cards' alignment axis (F74).
New (refines F24, F44, F74). *Why:* the matrix is the *honest* device for capability-heavy products (Synthesia, Webflow, Going, Mintlify, HubSpot all stack a full matrix under or instead of cards), but it's overkill — and visual noise — for a 3-tier product whose differences fit in six bullet lines (Sana, Going's card region). The checkmark is a *presence encoding*, the same way a status icon encodes state (F44): it must be consistent (one glyph for yes, one explicit glyph for no) and the columns must align cell-for-cell with the cards or the comparison breaks. A matrix with unaligned rows is a forbidden move — it's the comparison instrument failing at its one job.

**P-PR-08. Trust + FAQ sit near the decision, not only in the footer (the P-M-15 analog).** Directly under the card row: a one-line trust strip (compliance marks "SOC 2 / GDPR", payment-security badges, "No credit card required", a money-back line) and a short pricing-specific FAQ (3–6 questions: "Can I change plans later?", "What happens after the trial?", "Do you offer refunds?"). The FAQ pre-empts the objections that stall a purchase at the exact point they arise.
New (extends P-M-15). *Why:* the skeptical reader's last objections — *is my money safe, can I back out, what's the catch* — surface at the decision, so the answers belong there. Sana ("ISO 27001 certified, GDPR compliant" under the cards), monday (money-back + VISA/Norton/SSL badges by the CTA), Synthesia ("No credit card required" under the Free price). A trust strip relegated to the footer arrives after the reader has already hesitated.

**P-PR-09. Enterprise tier shows NO price — a "Custom" / "Let's talk" label where the number would be, a who-it's-for line about scale/security/compliance, a cumulative feature line ("Everything in Pro, plus:"), and a ghost "Contact sales" CTA.** It is the only tier whose CTA opens a conversation rather than a checkout, and it never carries the recommended-tier lift.
New. *Why:* enterprise pricing is negotiated, so a number would be a lie or a ceiling; the tier's job is to *qualify* the large buyer into a sales conversation. Sana ("Custom pricing / Contact sales"), Jasper ("Let's talk / Contact Sales"), monday ("Contact us"), Webflow ("Custom pricing"), Rows ("Send us an email") are unanimous. Putting a price on enterprise, or making it the filled-CTA hero, misreads who that tier is for.

**P-PR-10. The card row reads ACROSS as a comparison, so the cards share a common baseline grid: price on one horizontal line, the feature list starting on one line, the CTA pinned to a common bottom (cards equal-height, CTAs bottom-aligned even when feature lists differ in length).** This is the one place equal treatment is *correct* — the cards are comparison peers (like KPI tiles or process steps), not features refusing to rank (the AP5 exception). The *content* ranks them (price, lift); the *grid* keeps them comparable.
New (refines F22, F74; the AP5 "sequential peers" carve-out). *Why:* a reader compares tiers by sweeping horizontally — price-to-price, CTA-to-CTA. If the prices sit at different heights or the CTAs float at the bottom of ragged-length feature lists, every comparison is a re-hunt. Bottom-pinned CTAs on equal-height cards (Jasper, Sana, Webflow, monday) make the sweep free. Equal card *structure* is not the AP5 tell here — refusing to *lift the recommended tier* (P-PR-04) is.

**P-PR-11. Mobile (F50): the card row reflows to a single vertical stack, recommended tier FIRST (not in source/middle order), each card full-width; the billing toggle stays pinned above the stack; the comparison matrix becomes either a horizontally-scrollable table with a sticky first column OR an accordion per tier — never dropped (F50), never a sideways squeeze that crushes columns.**
Refines F50, F52. *Why:* three side-by-side cards can't survive a 375px viewport, and the recommended tier must lead the stack so the reader meets the recommendation first. A matrix is the hardest thing to reflow — the two honest moves are horizontal-scroll-with-sticky-label-column or per-tier accordion; squeezing four tier columns into 375px (the naive flex-wrap) crushes every cell below legibility. Toggle stays reachable above the stack (F52 touch target ≥44px).

## Composition defaults
- **Page band order**: page H1 + sub-deck (the value framing, marketing voice) → **billing toggle with savings cue** → **tier-card row** → **trust strip** → optional **comparison matrix** → **pricing FAQ** → CTA-recap + footer. The toggle directly precedes the cards it controls; trust and FAQ directly follow the decision.
- **Tier-card row**: 2–4 equal-height cards on a shared baseline grid (P-PR-10), recommended tier lifted (P-PR-04), one filled CTA among ghost CTAs (P-PR-06), CTAs bottom-pinned. Card inner padding 24–32px; card row max-width returns to the page's default container (F74).
- **Card anatomy**: name → price (hero) → period/currency (subordinate) → who-it's-for → feature delta list (✓ + label) → CTA. Badge above the name on the lifted tier only.
- **Comparison matrix** (when present): category-grouped rows, tier columns aligned to the cards, ✓ / explicit `—`, non-boolean values numeric-aligned (F24), hairline row separators only (F21).
- **Trust strip**: compliance/security marks + "no card required" / money-back, one line under the cards.
- **FAQ**: 3–6 pricing-specific Q&A, accordion or two-column list, near the decision.
- **Mobile** (F50): single stack, recommended first; toggle pinned above; matrix horizontal-scroll-with-sticky-column or accordion.

## Forbidden moves
- **5+ tiers in the card row** — the eye can't compare five across; the product hasn't picked an audience (P-PR-01; AP5 face).
- **Every tier's CTA an equal-weight filled accent button** — no chosen primary, the page abdicates its recommendation (P-PR-06; AP18 face).
- **A decorative rainbow per tier** — each card a different hue (Buy Me a Coffee's per-tier colored banners are the cautionary case). Color is one accent, spent on the *recommended* tier's lift, not a tier-coding palette (breaks F17/F19; caught in the wild — `evidence/pricing.md`).
- **A comparison matrix with unaligned rows / blank "no" cells** — columns that don't line up with the cards, or blank cells where `—` belongs (a blank reads as "unknown", not "absent"). The comparison instrument failing its one job (P-PR-07).
- **The price set at the same size as the tier name** — burying the one fact the reader came for (P-PR-03).
- **A billing toggle with no savings cue** — a switch with no reason to flip it (P-PR-05).
- **A priced enterprise tier**, or enterprise carrying the filled-CTA / recommended lift (P-PR-09).
- **Trust + FAQ only in the footer** — the objections arise at the decision; the answers must too (P-PR-08; P-M-15).
- **Texture on or behind the cards** — they're a comparison instrument; atmosphere belongs in the hero band only (F36; density-island rule).
- **A matrix squeezed side-by-side on mobile** instead of horizontal-scroll/accordion — crushed unreadable columns (P-PR-11; F50).
- **CTAs floating at the bottom of ragged-length feature lists** — breaks the horizontal CTA-to-CTA sweep (P-PR-10).
