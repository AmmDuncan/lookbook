# Pattern: Layout (composition grammar)

**Inherits:** all of `fundamentals.md` unless overridden below — especially F8–F12 (spacing/rhythm), F13–F14 (hierarchy), F22–F25 + **F74** (alignment spine), F26–F28 (density), F50–F53 (reflow).
**Cite as:** `P-LY-<nn>`.

> **Cross-cutting pattern, not a surface archetype.** Like `patterns/containers.md` (which container holds a flow) and `patterns/motion.md` (the time layer), this owns *one decision every other pattern reaches for*: **how to divide a blank page into regions and columns before any content goes in.** The surface patterns (dashboard, form, detail, marketing…) own *what* fills each region and at *what density*; this grammar owns the **skeleton** — the region set, the column split, the content measure, the whitespace rhythm, and how that skeleton reflows. It does **not** re-specify the mobile collapse (defer to `patterns/mobile.md`) or the overlay-vs-page choice (defer to `patterns/containers.md`). Read it whenever you start a page from nothing, or when "show me N directions" needs to generate *structurally distinct* layouts from one design language.

## Surface intent
Composition is the grammar between atoms and recipes. A blank page is divided into **regions** (header / nav / canvas / rail / footer), regions are sized by a **column split**, content inside a region is capped to a **measure**, and the whole is paced by a **whitespace rhythm**. Done well, the skeleton answers "where does my eye go first, and what supports it?" before a single word is read — and the *same* design language generates a centered doc, a sidebar app, an asymmetric hero, and a multi-rail dashboard by varying only **emphasis × region split × rhythm**, never the tokens. Done badly, every section invents its own width (F74/AP21), the page is one undifferentiated vertical stack (AP9), everything is centered with no spine (AP6), or three equal cards refuse to rank (AP5). The dynamic-generation insight: **the split is computed from the content's shape, not chosen by habit** — reading-not-scanning ⇒ single column; nav + work area ⇒ left rail; primary-with-support ⇒ main + right rail; two genuine equals ⇒ split; persistent nav + work + properties ⇒ three columns. Tally the shape; don't reach for the same grid every time.

## Density band
**Inherit the surface's own band — layout does not set density (F26–F28); it sets the skeleton the density fills.** A dashboard skeleton holds compact rows (P-D); a marketing skeleton holds airy sections; a settings skeleton holds medium fields. The one layout-level density rule: **one rhythm per surface, held** (P-LY-09). Page margins and section gaps below scale with the surface's band, not independently.

## The vocabulary (define once, reuse)
- **Container measures** — a small sanctioned set (F74), each a max-width the content returns to:
  - `prose` ~680–720px (≤75ch, F5) — articles, docs, long-form, settings forms, auth cards, modal bodies.
  - `app` ~1200–1280px — most product surfaces (detail pages, forms-in-shell, list+rail).
  - `wide` ~1440–1536px — dashboards, data tables, multi-rail tools.
  - `full-bleed` — edge-to-edge backgrounds only; inner content **returns to one of the above**.
- **Grid** — 12 columns, gutter = one space token (`--space-4` / 16px, scaling up a step at `wide`). Spans of **12 · 6+6 · 8+4 · 4+4+4 · 3×4** cover ~90% of cases; odd spans (5, 7) read as accidents (P-D-08 echo).
- **Rail** — a fixed-width supporting column, **~320px** default (a card + padding), **~280px** when dense/text-only; over ~360px it is a second column, not a rail.
- **Rhythm scale** (between sections) — `tight` 32 · `comfortable` 48 · `airy` 80 · `hero-airy` 96 (all `--space-*`). Inside a card/group: `--space-3`–`--space-6`. **Section gap and inside-card gap are different scales — never the same value** (P-LY-08).

## Calibrations

**P-LY-01. Pick the region set from the content's shape, not by habit — it reduces to six canonical splits.** *Single column* (12) when reading > scanning (prose, docs, walkthroughs). *Main + right rail* (8/4) when primary content drives and secondary supports (TOC, summary, related, properties). *Main + left rail* (4/8) when the rail is **navigation**, not content (settings, docs, admin). *Two equal* (6/6) for two genuine equals (auth split, compare, side-by-side editor). *Asymmetric* (7/5) for a hero with media on one side — priority without shouting. *Three column* (nav · main · rail) only for app shells where all three are **persistent** surfaces (IDE, design tool, email, issue tracker).
New (formalizes gallery → Region splits). *Why:* the split is a *reading-order* decision — it sets where the eye lands and what's secondary (F13 hierarchy by position). Reaching for the same two-column hero or three-card row every time is the genericness AP7/AP5/AP9 catch; choosing by shape is the proof the page was reasoned about. *Check:* name the region set and the one sentence of content-shape that chose it.

**P-LY-02. Left rail = navigation / "where I am"; right rail = supporting content / "what's nearby". Never swap them.** Persistent app nav, settings sections, doc trees go **left**; summary, properties, TOC, related, activity, checkout total go **right**. Readers learn left = location, right = context.
Refines: F13, F22. *Why:* the convention is near-universal in shipped products (the eye's F/Z scan depends on it). A summary on the left or nav on the right forces relearning on every page. *Check:* every rail's job is classifiable as nav-or-support, and it sits on the matching side.

**P-LY-03. The app shell is a persistent left sidebar (grouped nav), not a top nav bar, for product surfaces with >~5 destinations.** Marketing/auth/single-flows keep a top bar; product apps (dashboard, detail, list, settings) lead with a left rail. A pinned account/upgrade/trial card at the rail's bottom is a real, good pattern.
Refines: P-D composition default; generalizes it past dashboards. *Why:* a left rail scales to many grouped destinations and frees the top edge for page-level scope/actions; a top bar caps at ~5–7 items before it crowds. Every studied real product app used a left rail (`evidence/layout.md`). *Check:* product surface with a deep nav uses a left sidebar; the top edge carries page scope, not the global nav.

**P-LY-04. Cap content to a measure; let the container be wider than the text. Body/prose at 60–75ch (~680–720px max-width) even inside a `wide` shell.** A region can be 900px wide while its paragraph column is 680px — that is correct, not a bug. Settings forms, descriptions, and reading content take the prose measure *nested inside* the app/wide region.
Refines: F5, F74. *Why:* line length is set by reading ergonomics, not by the column it sits in (F5). Stretching text the full width of a wide container is the most common measure failure. Real settings pages (Squarespace, Lindy) nest a ~640px form inside a much wider content region (`evidence/layout.md`). *Check:* prose/form `max-width` resolves to ~60–75ch regardless of its region's width.

**P-LY-05. All section measures reduce to ≤3 sanctioned widths on ONE shared axis (the F74 spine), and narrower measures NEST inside and align to the wider one's edge/center.** Don't invent a per-section width; don't flip between left-flush and centered down the page. Full-bleed backgrounds span edge to edge, but their inner content returns to the set and to the same axis.
Refines: F74. *Why:* this is the alignment spine — the difference between "one designed layout" and "stacked fragments." It hides from the contrast/copy/AP sweeps because each section looks fine alone; the break only shows when you trace the left edge down the page (AP21). *Check:* list every section's content width — ≤3 measures, one axis; trace the left (and right) edge top-to-bottom — narrower sits *inside* wider, never off to its own side.

**P-LY-06. Whitespace is the loudest hierarchy signal — bigger gap above a heading than below it, so heading + body group by proximity. Spacing does more hierarchy work than font size.** Between distinct sections: 32–96px by surface band (the rhythm scale). Below 32px between sections, readers stop reading them as sections.
Refines: F11, F12, F13. *Why:* the gestalt law of proximity — what's close belongs together (F12). Uniform gaps everywhere (AP8) destroy grouping; relying on size alone for hierarchy (AP-adjacent) underuses the cheapest tool. *Check:* the gap above a heading exceeds the gap below it; between-section gap is in the surface's band; no two unrelated groups share an equal gap with a related pair.

**P-LY-07. Asymmetry signals priority; symmetry signals equality — choose deliberately.** Reach for an **asymmetric** split (7/5, 8/4, or a left-flush hero) when one side leads and the other supports — it commits to a reading axis (F23). Reserve **symmetric** (6/6, centered) for two genuine equals or a short isolated display moment (≤4 words, F23). Centered-everything down the whole page is the no-spine tell (AP6); a row of equal-weight *features* refusing to rank is AP5 (numbered *steps* and peer KPI tiles are legitimately equal — not the tell).
Refines: F23, F25; ties AP5/AP6/AP20. *Why:* a 7/5 hero says "this side matters more" without a heavy hand; a 6/6 split says "these are peers." Defaulting to centered/symmetric is the safe-everywhere regression (AP20) — it never commits. Real marketing heroes are asymmetric (Otter 7/5 text/media, Ferndesk ~45/55, Relevance left-flush — `evidence/layout.md`). *Check:* every split is justifiable as "one leads" (asymmetric) or "genuine equals / display moment" (symmetric); the page is not centered top-to-bottom.

**P-LY-08. Section rhythm and inside-card rhythm are two different scales — pick one section rhythm per surface and hold it; never let the between-section gap equal the inside-card gap.** `tight` (32) for dense admin, `comfortable` (48) for default product, `airy` (80) for marketing, `hero-airy` (96) for launch heroes; inside cards/forms stay `--space-3`–`--space-6`.
Refines: F11, F12, F28. *Why:* when the gap between sections equals the gap inside a card, the cards visually merge into the page and the section structure collapses. One held rhythm is what makes a multi-section page read as one system rather than assembled bands (AP9). *Check:* section gap ≥ ~2× the inside-card gap; one section-rhythm value across the surface.

**P-LY-09. Auto-fit grids for unknown/equivalent item counts; fixed columns when "N across" is part of the story.** `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(~220–280px, 1fr))` for card grids, galleries, search results, project lists — they reshape with the container, no breakpoints. Use **fixed** `repeat(N, 1fr)` + reflow for a headline KPI row or hero feature grid where 4-up is intentional (then 4→2→1, never 4→1). Cap a KPI hero at 4 (P-D-04).
Refines: F50, F51; generalizes P-D-04/09. *Why:* auto-fit handles the case a fixed grid can't (unknown count) and removes breakpoint guesswork; fixed columns preserve a deliberate "4 across" that auto-fit would break. Real dashboards use 2-up card pairs and auto-fitting tile rows, not a rigid 12-col everywhere (`evidence/layout.md`). *Check:* equivalent-unknown sets use auto-fit; meaningful-fixed counts use fixed columns with a stated reflow.

**P-LY-10. Reflow is mobile-first and content-driven: defer the collapse rules to `patterns/mobile.md`, but every split states where and how it stacks — never a direct N-col→1-col jump, never silently `display:none` a content column.** Right rail drops below main (or into a tab); left nav becomes an icon-rail then a drawer; three-column → two → drawer → single. Sticky is dropped on mobile except a single primary action footer (P-LY-11).
Refines: F50–F53; defers to `patterns/mobile.md`. *Why:* a split that doesn't declare its collapse is unfinished the way a data view without an empty state is (F33). Hiding a content column below a breakpoint breaks content parity (F50) — if it mattered, surface it; if it didn't, cut it everywhere. *Check:* each region split has a stated reflow at ~768 and ~390; no content-bearing column is `display:none`; no jump skips the intermediate stack.

**P-LY-11. Pin a region only when it's short and supporting; never pin main content, anything taller than ~70vh, or anything on mobile.** Right rails (summary, TOC, related, checkout total) and a form/checkout action footer may stick; the main scroll column and the nav body do not. On mobile, drop sticky except the one primary action footer.
New. *Why:* a pinned element that exceeds the viewport traps its own overflow and eats the scroll; pinning the main column defeats reading. Sticky on a phone steals thumb-zone real estate. *Check:* sticky elements are ≤70vh and supporting; mobile keeps at most the primary action footer pinned.

## Composition defaults
- **Start from the region set** (P-LY-01) chosen by content shape, then size it on the 12-col grid (P-LY-05), then cap each region's content to a measure (P-LY-04), then set one section rhythm (P-LY-08).
- **Spine first:** decide the shared axis and ≤3 measures before placing sections; trace the left edge as you go (P-LY-05/F74).
- **Rails:** ~320px (280 if dense); left = nav, right = support (P-LY-02); pin only short supporting rails (P-LY-11).
- **App shell:** persistent left sidebar for deep-nav product surfaces, top bar for marketing/auth/single-flow (P-LY-03); account/scope card at rail bottom.
- **Grids:** auto-fit for unknown counts, fixed for deliberate N-across (P-LY-09); spans of 12/6+6/8+4/4+4+4/3×4, no odd spans.
- **Rhythm:** one section rhythm held, ≥2× the inside-card gap (P-LY-06/08).
- **Three directions from one language:** vary **emphasis × region split × section rhythm** only — keep components, tokens, and type scale identical (gallery → "One page, three directions"). This is the sanctioned way to make pages feel distinct without breaking consistency.
- **Reflow + container:** every split declares its collapse (defer to `patterns/mobile.md`); the overlay-vs-page question defers to `patterns/containers.md`.

## Forbidden moves
- **Every section a different, unrelated width off its own axis** — the wandering-edge spine break (AP21 / F74; P-LY-05).
- **Text stretched the full width of a wide container** — cap it; let the container be wider than the text (P-LY-04 / F5).
- **Centered everything, top to bottom** — no reading spine; reserve center for short display moments (AP6; P-LY-07).
- **Three equal-weight feature cards refusing to rank** (AP5) — vary size/composition; *steps and peer tiles are exempt* (P-LY-07).
- **Nav on the right or a summary on the left** — swapped rail roles (P-LY-02).
- **The endless identical vertical stack** — same band shape repeated with no pacing (AP9; vary rhythm/measure — P-LY-06/08).
- **Section gap = inside-card gap** — cards merge into the page, structure collapses (P-LY-08).
- **A rail wider than ~360px** (it's a second column) or a top nav forced onto a deep-nav product app (P-LY-02/03).
- **A direct N-col → 1-col jump, or a content column `display:none` on mobile** — breaks the reflow ladder / content parity (P-LY-10 / F50).
- **Pinning the main column, a >70vh element, or anything but the action footer on mobile** (P-LY-11).
- **A two-column split of body copy** (print, not screen — readers lose which column continues) and **odd grid spans** (5/7 read as accidents).
