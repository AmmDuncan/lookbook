Lookbook · Chapter 07

Identity.

A brand isn't a logo and tokens aren't a brand. This chapter is the bridge — how a client's wordmark, voice, and visual personality translate into the system underneath. Get this seam right and the system disappears; get it wrong and every project looks like the same template wearing a different hat.

01 — Wordmark

Wordmark anatomy #wordmark

The wordmark is the first decision. Six properties to set deliberately — never let them be defaults.

Acme
A — Typeface

Display, not body

Choose a display cut, not the body weight. The body font in the system covers UI; the wordmark gets a distinct cut chosen for confidence at one specific size.

B — Weight

Semibold, almost always

Light feels luxe but disappears at small sizes (favicon). Bold feels loud at large sizes (hero). Semibold survives both. Pick light only if the brand truly is luxe.

C — Tracking

Tighter than UI

Body type tracks at 0 to -0.02em. Wordmark tracks at -0.025 to -0.035em. The tighter spacing reads as "designed" instead of "typed."

D — Casing

Capitalize the first letter only

"Acme" not "ACME" — unless the brand is genuinely shout-y (industrial, regulated, performance). All-caps wordmarks read as 1990s. Lowercase reads as 2014.

E — Optical pairs

Kern by hand

Most automated kerning lies. Walk through the actual letters: Av, Wa, Ty, fi. If two letters touch awkwardly, fix them. The wordmark is one specific arrangement — not a font sample.

F — Companion glyph

Optional, but earn it

A glyph buys you favicon, app icon, and social avatar in one move. But a bad glyph is worse than no glyph. If you can't justify the shape, drop it and use the wordmark's initial instead.

03 — Clear space

The margin around the mark #clearspace

Define clear space as a multiple of the mark's own height — never in pixels. That way the rule scales when the lockup does. Use 1× minimum, 2× preferred.

Acme
x x x x
x = mark height

In the diagram above, the mark's glyph is 44px. Clear space is then 44px (1x) on every side — the minimum any other element can approach. Preferred is 88px (2x).

When to enforce

Hard rule on partner lockups, press kits, and external surfaces. Soft rule in your own site nav, where 1x is usually impractical — at 0.5x it still reads as a logo, not a label.

04 — Context

The mark in four environments #context

The mark needs to survive light, dark, color, and imagery. Design every variant up front and put them in a shared kit — not "we'll figure it out for the dark theme later."

Acme
Acme
Acme
Acme
On light
Mark in --text-primary. Default. Test on --bg AND --surface-sunken — the sunken case is where a too-light glyph disappears.
On dark
Mark in white, not --text-primary (which is off-white on dark). Pure white earns the contrast and reads as confident. Glyph's internal cut switches to dark background.
On accent
Mark in white. Glyph in white with the accent showing through the cut. Test AA contrast against the accent — if it fails, the accent is too bright for this purpose; create a darker brand-only accent.
On image
White mark with a subtle scrim behind. Never just slap white on a photo — add a 12% black gradient or a 6% blur so the mark survives any future image swap. The scrim is part of the lockup.

06 — Voice

Brand voice as two axes #voice

Voice usually fails because it's described in adjectives ("confident, friendly, modern") that don't survive contact with a microcopy decision. Pick a point on two axes and write a sample sentence for each surface. Then the agent — human or otherwise — can match it.

Formal Playful
Error We couldn't sign you in. Check your password and try again.
Empty No projects yet. Create one to get started.
Terse Warm
Success Saved. Your changes are live.
Welcome Welcome back, Andrea. Pick up where you left off.
The same message at three points on the formal ↔ playful axis
Formal
Your account has been successfully created.
Default · neutral
You're in. Andrea, your workspace is ready.
Playful
You're in! 🎉 Let's get you started.
Pick a column up front. The middle column is the default; the others are deliberate. Never mix all three on one site.

Design on a 22% corner-radius square (iOS curve). Center the glyph at 60–70% of the square's width.
Don't design edge-to-edge. The OS may letterbox or crop and the visual weight breaks.
08 — What not to do

The seven misuses #misuse

Every brand guide has these. Most are ignored because they feel obvious — until you see them in a partner email or a press kit. Write them down anyway.

Acme
Don't skew, rotate, or distort the mark.
Acme
Don't place on uncontrolled gradients or busy color.
Acme
Don't add shadows, glows, bevels, or strokes.
Acme
Don't recolor the mark outside the approved set.
Acme
Don't change the spacing between glyph and wordmark.
Acme
Don't re-typeset the wordmark in another font.

Brand sits above tokens.

Identity is what makes two sites built on the same system feel like two different companies. Map the mark into the contract once — then let Personality, Motion, and Imagery do their work.
