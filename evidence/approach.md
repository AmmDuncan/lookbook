# Evidence — Approach (the opening move) (P-AP calibration)

Method: Approach is **strategy, not a surface**, so this is a lighter, reverse-engineering study rather than a measure-the-pixels calibration. I pulled real, public, shipped products from **four deliberately different domains** (fintech, creative/design tool, developer tool, consumer-social) and read each one backwards: *what job is this surface optimizing for, and what archetype / density / tone / dial-set / signature did the team choose to serve it — and why does that fit the domain?* The goal was to test whether the gallery's Approach framework (job → density/archetype → brand→dials → one signature) **explains real divergence** between products built from broadly similar component kits. It does — cleanly enough that each screen maps to a brand-archetype column with five dials in-column and one wildcard. Studied against real public products; the aggregator used to find them is not a calibration source and is deliberately unnamed. 2026-05-29.

Screens — **Fintech:** Monzo (coral brand balance-card pinned above a calm white transaction feed), Starling Bank (neutral list, date-grouped income/payment rows, mono-ish amounts), Monarch (deep-blue balance hero + recent-transactions + bottom tab nav). **Creative / design tool:** Figma (near-mono 3-panel canvas with layers rail + properties rail, mono numbers, accent only on selection + Share), Framer (same canvas-with-rails shape, accent on Publish), Visual Electric (image-led generative canvas, type whispers). **Developer tool:** Replit (split agent/workspace with a **locked-dark log pane**, monospace logs, status-only green/red), Render (service detail + locked-dark live-log terminal, "Live" status pill). **Consumer-social:** Retro (friendly serif title, warm rounded photo-grid feed cards), Weverse (saturated gradient profile hero, playful badge glyphs, bold display name), Posts (clean text-led feed, generous imagery, FAB compose). 2026-05-29.

## The dynamic-generation lesson
The four domains are built from broadly the same primitive vocabulary (cards, lists, tabs, fields, status pills) yet look nothing alike — and the *reason* is exactly the Approach chain: **job → density/archetype → dials → one signature.** Monzo and Figma could be assembled from the same kit; the *job* ("glance at money" vs "maximize the canvas") is why one is a warm card-over-feed and the other a near-mono dual-rail tool. Every screen resolved to a brand-archetype column with **five dials in-column and one wildcard** — Monzo's saturated balance card, Replit's strict status-only colour discipline — which is the observable, shipped form of P-AP-05/06. This is the proof that Approach is a *decision framework that produces divergence*, not a style: pick the job honestly and the archetype, density, dials, and signature mostly fall out; the one wildcard is where the product earns its fingerprint.

## Over-strict / wrong-rule catches (calibrations applied)
- **The gallery frames Approach as client/marketing-site work** (a 90-minute call, exactly 12 questions in order, scope tiers, sitemaps). Real in-app surfaces (Figma, Replit) showed the load-bearing inputs are smaller and survive at every scale: *job, one primary action, archetype, guardrail*. → **Calibrated P-AP-02**: match discovery depth to engagement — full brief for greenfield, compressed brief for an in-app flow (inherit the app's existing dials, don't re-derive). Approach explicitly extends to in-app flows (Mode B), not just new sites.
- **The gallery leads with "brand archetype" as the first big lever.** The strongest *divergence-driver* observed was the **job-to-be-done + content shape**, with brand layered on top — a fintech dashboard and a fintech marketing page share a brand but not a density. → **Calibrated P-AP-03**: derive density + archetype from the job/content FIRST, apply the brand dials AFTER (and let the job's density override the column default). This keeps the F26 density-from-the-work discipline at the front, where the #1 generic tell is prevented.
- **Tool surfaces (Figma/Framer/Replit) have essentially no signature and that's correct.** A naive "always add a signature move" would have flagged them. → **Reinforced P-AP-07 / AP20 scope**: on scan-first product surfaces the signature is the one *allowed* flourish, not a per-region demand; restraint there is the goal, not timidity.
- **The wildcard (a borrowed dial) and the signature (a Variation composition play) are two different levers.** Monzo's saturated balance card is a *dial-level* wildcard (accent intensity); Visual Electric's image-taller-than-type is a *composition-level* signature. → **Kept distinct in P-AP-06** so projects don't conflate them into a costume.

## Calibrations applied to patterns/approach.md
1. **Job first** — one-sentence job-to-be-done is the load-bearing input, stated before any composition (P-AP-01).
2. **Right-weight brief** — full 12-question brief for greenfield; compressed (job + primary action + archetype + guardrail) for in-app flows (P-AP-02).
3. **Density + archetype from the job/content, not the brand** — brand applied on top; job's density can override the column default (P-AP-03).
4. **One named primary action + metric** per surface; never two co-equal (P-AP-04).
5. **Six dials, five-in-column + one wildcard** from the brand-archetype table; hands accent/display-font/radius + voice to Identity (P-AP-05).
6. **Wildcard (a dial) ≠ signature (a Variation play)** — one of each, kept distinct (P-AP-06).
7. **One signature, chosen at approach-time, built last, removable**; restrained on scan-first surfaces (P-AP-07).
8. **Four-lens audit before redesigning** an inherited surface (P-AP-08).
9. **Write the readout** (kickoff one-pager) and hold every decision against it; the embarrassment guardrail is the most valuable line (P-AP-09).
10. **Readout feeds the 2–3 directions** — invariants fixed by Approach, composables (emphasis/split/rhythm) varied by composition (P-AP-10).
