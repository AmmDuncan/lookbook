# Evidence — Pricing (P-PR calibration)

Method: studied 13 real web pricing pages across SaaS, creator-platform, video, CRM, and dev-tool domains, read by **(a) craft to learn** and **(b) how the rules are applied** — where reality confirms a rule, calibrates a number, or breaks/bounds a rule (→ rule is wrong / too strict / needs a cap). The strongest calibrations are the rules real screens *bent* or the tell caught in the wild. Pricing inherits the marketing surface (spacious, show-don't-tell, trust-near-decision) but its core device is a comparison instrument — closer to a small data table than a hero. 2026-05-29.

Screens: Jasper (Choose a plan — 3 tiers), Sana AI (Pricing — 3 tiers, two captures: dark landing + light modal), HoneyBook (Membership — 3 tiers, toggle), Going (Compare Plans — 3 tiers + matrix, illustration), Synthesia (Compare plans — 4 tiers + full matrix), monday.com (Choose a plan — 4 tiers, badges), Webflow (Workspace plans — 4 tiers + matrix, two captures), Rows (Select a Plan — 4 tiers), Deezer (2 tiers, toggle), Ghost (2 tiers, annual switch), Mintlify (settings billing matrix, 3 tiers), HubSpot (Marketing — 3 tiers + matrix + compare-select), Buy Me a Coffee (membership levels — the cautionary rainbow-per-tier case).

## The dynamic-generation lesson
Pricing pages vary by **how much the tiers differ and who the product is for**: a simple consumer/creator product (Deezer, Ghost) needs 2 tiers + a toggle and no matrix; a 3-tier SaaS whose deltas fit six lines (Sana, Going's card region) is cards-only; a capability-heavy product (Synthesia, Webflow, HubSpot) stacks a full feature matrix under the cards; an enterprise-targeting product adds a priceless "contact us" 4th tier. The constants are the *reading order* (price → who-it's-for → delta → one CTA) and the *one editorial decision* (lift the recommended tier); the **tier count, matrix-or-not, and toggle flex to the product's shape.** Getting cards-vs-matrix right — and lifting exactly one tier — is the proof the page was reasoned from the offering, not stamped from a template.

## Over-strict / wrong-rule catches (the strongest calibrations)
- **Webflow filled TWO paid CTAs** (Core + Growth) for a deliberate two-step upgrade ladder — showed "one filled CTA" needs a contextual carve-out: a page MAY weight two adjacent paid tiers when pushing an upgrade ladder, but never all, and the recommended one still reads primary via badge+lift. Softened a naive "exactly one filled" into P-PR-06's nuance.
- **The equal-card grid is the AP5 *exception*, not the tell** — pricing cards are comparison peers (like KPI tiles), so equal structure + bottom-pinned CTAs is *correct*; the AP5 tell is refusing to *lift the recommended tier*. This is the inverse of the marketing read (where equal feature cards ARE the tell) — same rule, opposite verdict by surface. Fed P-PR-10.
- **Buy Me a Coffee's rainbow-per-tier** proved tier-coding-by-hue is a real shipped tell (breaks F17/F19) — fed the forbidden move; calibrated against monday's single thin accent top-rule (borderline-OK because it's one accent strip, not a per-card palette).
- **HubSpot/Synthesia explicit `—`/✗ for absent features** showed a blank cell reads as "unknown", not "no" — fed P-PR-07's "never a blank 'no' cell".

## Calibrations applied to patterns/pricing.md
1. Tier count → **2–4, three canonical, four only with Enterprise; 5+ forbidden** (P-PR-01).
2. Card anatomy → **name → price → who-it's-for → feature *delta* → one CTA** (P-PR-02).
3. Price → **hero of the card, 40–64px, period/currency subordinate, struck original muted** (P-PR-03).
4. Recommended tier → **lift by stacked cues (badge + border + tint + scale + only-filled CTA)** — the AP5 answer (P-PR-04).
5. Toggle → **above the row, savings cue always attached** (P-PR-05).
6. CTAs → **one filled among ghosts; two-paid-tier ladder allowed; enterprise ghost; current plan disabled pill** (P-PR-06).
7. Matrix → **contextual, below the cards, ✓/explicit-`—`, columns aligned, never blank "no"** (P-PR-07).
8. Trust + FAQ → **at the decision, not only the footer** (P-PR-08).
9. Enterprise → **no price, "Custom"/"Contact sales", never the lifted tier** (P-PR-09).
10. Grid → **equal-height, bottom-pinned CTAs — the AP5 comparison-peer carve-out** (P-PR-10).
11. Mobile → **single stack recommended-first; toggle pinned; matrix scroll/accordion, never squeezed** (P-PR-11).

### Over-strict / wrong-rule catches this run
- **Slack's blank "no" cells** are the one place reality bends a stated rule (P-PR-07 / the "never blank" forbidden move). A shipped, excellent product uses blank cells in a *category-grouped* matrix where the grouping makes blank unambiguous (the lower tier simply has nothing in that category). The explicit-glyph rule is still the better default (Elicit/HubSpot prove it reads cleaner) — so this is downgraded in spirit from "absolute" to "strong preference," but **not changed in the file**: the rule as written produces the better screen, and one counterexample doesn't clear the battle-test bar to loosen a forbidden move. Parked for a future run if a second strong product repeats it.
- **Frame.io's ringed Enterprise** tested P-PR-09's "enterprise never carries the lift" and the rule survived — the ring is plan-picker selection state, not a recommended badge. The distinction (selection affordance ≠ recommendation lift) is worth keeping in mind but is not a rule gap.
