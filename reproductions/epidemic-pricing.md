> Deepening pass: pricing is the highest slop-risk surface in the queue (three-identical-cards, badge soup) — the strongest test of whether the base *resists* slop, not just reproduces a clean screen.

## The decisions I extracted (not traced)

1. **One loud move: a centered editorial serif headline.** "30-day free trial. Cancel anytime." in a display serif — typographic courage where most pricing pages default to a plain sans. The §3 pairing: one display serif (Newsreader) + one neutral sans (Hanken Grotesk) for everything else.
2. **Tier differentiation by restraint, not a badge.** Three tiers, but Enterprise is *structurally* different — no price ("Request a quote"), a near-black CTA instead of the pink. No "MOST POPULAR" banner. The differentiation is editorial, which is the §11 antidote to three-identical-cards *without* reaching for a loud highlight.
3. **One accent (pink) on the primary CTAs; Enterprise CTA goes dark.** §4 60-30-10: the brand pink is spent only on the two "Start free trial" buttons; the Enterprise "Contact sales" is near-black so it reads as a different kind of action.
4. **Pricing-specific trust + microcopy.** "Save X% by paying yearly" under each price, a "100% secure payments" strip with payment wordmarks. These are the signals that de-risk a purchase decision.
5. **Per-tier feature lists that build.** "Everything in Personal, plus:" / "Everything in Commercial, plus:" — the cumulative-value pattern, base tier has a plain checklist (no "plus" header).

## Where the base HELPED

- **§3 Type** — "typographic courage… one display/serif + one neutral sans." The serif headline + sans body pairing is straight from here.
- **§12-B Marketing playbook** — pricing in the section structure, "one primary CTA," generous whitespace, imagery/voice earns its place.
- **§11 Slop avoidance** — "three identical centered cards… give them real differentiation." The restrained Enterprise-differentiation is the fix, applied.
- **§4 Color** — one accent on the CTA, semantic restraint, tinted off-white canvas.

## Where the base was SILENT (the distillable)

1. **No pricing-table recipe.** §12-B *names* pricing as a section but gives no how-to: the 3-tier grid, the monthly/yearly toggle, the cumulative "everything in X, plus" lists, the two ways to differentiate (lift-the-recommended-tier *or* editorial-restraint + a structurally-different enterprise), one-accent-CTA + dark-enterprise-CTA, the trust strip. This is a high-frequency, high-slop-risk surface and the base has no recipe. → **cookbook candidate: "the pricing table (3 tiers, toggle, differentiate by restraint or lift, trust strip)."**
