Composed Recipe · Settings · one skin, five compositions

Same skin. Five ways to lay out settings.

One settings surface — profile, notifications, billing, security — composed five ways. Every frame wears the same neutral skin: off-white page, white surfaces with hairline borders, slightly-rounded corners, one restrained blue accent, system sans with tabular figures. Toggles render a clearly visible sliding thumb (left = off, right = on). What changes is purely composition and interaction.

Composition 1
Section nav + form

PULLS — a left settings nav (Profile / Account / Notifications / Billing / Security) + right grouped field sections + a sticky bottom Save bar (Save / Cancel).  WHY — the standard settings layout: a persistent nav scopes a large surface into chapters, and a sticky save bar makes pending changes unmissable.

Composition 2
Tabbed settings

PULLS — horizontal tabs across the top + grouped setting cards below.  WHY — for a flatter surface with a handful of areas: tabs read as peers and keep the content full-width, which suits cards better than a narrow nav column.

Composition 3
Single scroll + anchored nav

PULLS — a long single-page settings layout with a sticky left anchored sub-nav (scroll-spy highlights the active section).  WHY — when settings are read more than navigated: one continuous page lets users skim everything, while the outline tracks position and jumps on click.

Composition 4
Preferences toggles

PULLS — rows of preference toggles grouped by category, each row a label + description + a toggle with a visible thumb, mixing on and off states.  WHY — for notification-style settings that are almost all booleans: a clean grouped list of toggles is the most direct mapping of “turn this on or off.”

Composition 5
Danger zone

PULLS — an account section + a clearly separated danger-zone card (Delete account, Export data) with a confirm affordance.  WHY — destructive actions need distance and friction: a red-bordered zone set apart from normal settings, plus a typed-confirm step, prevents costly mistakes.

Apply
Skins & how to apply them

A skin is the full set of visual tokens — color, type, radius, elevation, and density — applied uniformly. Pick ONE skin per product and apply it across whichever compositions you choose: the composition is the skeleton, the skin is the paint, so any composition above renders coherently in any skin. Every frame on this page uses the Neutral column.
